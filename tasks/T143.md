## Task
ADR-0029 U9 (`docs/adr/0029-kernel-range-and-time-model.md:397–399`):
"`show_odds` displays the outcome distribution from a server-side read-only
preview query (Follow-on 10), never client math". Follow-on 10 (`:489`,
`docs/IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md:217`) makes it this task: "`crpg-sim`, read-only
action preview: exact integer outcome distribution, bounded (U9)".

Build `preview_action(world, actor, ability, target)`. For one candidate
`UseAbility` it returns, for every outcome of the ability's table, how many
of the equally likely attack draws decide that outcome, how many of those a
natural-face rule decided, and the exact distribution of the damage that
outcome deals. Every probability is an integer count over a stated integer
total. The function takes `&World`, so it mutates nothing and draws nothing
from the world's RNG. It refuses, rather than approximates, when the exact
distribution is larger than a stated bound.

The preview is the real resolution enumerated, not a second rules model. It
runs `validate_action` first, builds the same `ActionPlan` that execution
uses (`crates/crpg-sim/src/combat.rs:3229`), takes the defence value from
the kernel's own `resolve`, and folds every possible roll through the
kernel's `ModifierPipeline::query_numeric` and `OutcomeTable::evaluate`.
The only arithmetic it repeats is the dice themselves (sum, keep/drop,
offset, clamp) and the damage clamp. A seed sweep test compares it with
`perform_action` draw for draw (G1–G6), so a drift fails a test.

## Readings
What the code does today, each checked at `095ed20`.

1. **Execution path.** An accepted turn-based `UseAbility` runs
   `resolve_use` (`crates/crpg-sim/src/combat.rs:3899`). It builds the
   `ActionPlan` (`:3914–3916`), resolves through the kernel's `resolve` on
   `combat.roll` (`:3955`, `:3978`), then draws the damage dice with
   `damage_for` (`:3981`, `:4034–4050`). Real-time due orders run the same
   `resolve_use` from `execution_stage` (`:4520`, `:4580`), so the two
   modes resolve alike (T138 changes when, not how).
2. **The plan.** `action_plan` (`:3229–3423`) builds the pipeline, both
   views (stats, tags, modifiers), the bonus-stat synthetic modifiers and the
   `Against` for all five defences, from a scratch interner. It reads the
   world and writes nothing.
3. **Its one world write is outside the plan.** For a `TargetStat` defence,
   `resolve_use` interns the stat into the world interner before cloning it
   (`:3911–3913`, T137 B10). Inside the plan the stat is always interned in
   the scratch clone too, because it is a usable stat (T137 B6), so a plan
   built without that write gives the same values (B11).
4. **Legality.** `validate_action` (`:3448`) is the one legality source
   (`crates/crpg-sim/AGENTS.md:269`, invariant 12): "Never re-check admission
   in a second predicate or validate by executing on a clone". Its last
   stages are T137's stat evaluation (`combat.rs:3611–3615`) and the range
   stage.
5. **Resolution order.** `resolve` (`crates/crpg-rules/src/resolution.rs:480`)
   evaluates a non-opposed defence in preflight before any draw (`:563`),
   draws the actor's dice and then the opposing dice on one staged RNG,
   folds each raw total through `query_numeric(…, ModifierTarget::Roll(…),
   raw, view)`, and decides with `outcome_table.evaluate(margin,
   actor_natural)` (`:703–704`). A side with `RollSpec::None` has raw total
   zero and touches no stream (`:273`); a resolution that draws nothing
   leaves the RNG unchanged (`:705–707`).
6. **Natural faces.** `RollRequest.natural_die` selects a raw draw index,
   "even when that die is later dropped" (`resolution.rs:288–293`). The
   opposing roll of `Opposed` has `natural_die: None` (`combat.rs:3385`, T137
   B13). `OutcomeTable::evaluate` (`resolution.rs:197–222`) applies at most
   one matching rule; a face that matches no rule gives the band's outcome and
   `natural_rule_index: None`.
7. **Dice.** `DiceExpr::evaluate` (`crates/crpg-rules/src/dice.rs:142–170`)
   draws `count` faces uniform on `1..=sides`, keeps by `selection`
   (`:197–249`), and clamps kept sum plus offset to `i32` once (`:168`). The
   face draw is `gen_range_u32`, which redraws below a threshold
   to avoid modulo bias (`crates/crpg-core/src/rng.rs:74–84`). So every face
   tuple is equally likely, and counting tuples is the exact distribution.
8. **Damage.** `damage_for` takes the first flat entry for the outcome or 0,
   adds the first damage-dice entry's total for that outcome when there is
   one, in `i64`, and clamps once into `0..=u32::MAX`; an outcome without
   dice draws nothing (`combat.rs:4034–4050`, T137 B16).
9. **Bounds of the input.** A dice expression has at most `MAX_DICE_COUNT`
   = 1,024 dice of at most `MAX_DIE_SIDES` = 1,000,000 sides
   (`crates/crpg-rules/src/lib.rs:66`, `:68`); a table has at most 256
   bands and 256 natural rules (`:70`, `:72`). An exhaustive distribution is
   therefore unbounded without a cap of its own.
10. **Who would read it.** `UiAbility.show_odds` is presentation data that
    the server ignores (U1,
    `docs/adr/0029-kernel-range-and-time-model.md:353–355`;
    `tasks/T142.md:253`). Protocol v3 carries no roll or preview message
    (T140 question 1, answered (a), `tasks/T140.md:1710–1724`). No crate
    calls anything like a preview today.

## Decisions
Decisions already made, which this contract follows:
- **U9 and Follow-on 10** (ADR-0029, accepted D30c, 2026-10-21): the odds
  come from a server-side, read-only query in `crpg-sim`, as an exact
  integer distribution, bounded; never client math. Not S-set (the row's S
  column is "—", `:489`).
- **T137's hand-off** (`tasks/T137.md:821–822`): the preview "must model
  `Opposed` and damage-dice draws, and the B15 stage". B4, B6, B8 and B1 do.
- **T134 stays deferred** (D33, T137 open question 1a): no data shape selects
  a counting mode, so there is no mode to preview. Exploding dice and glitch
  rules are deferred too (D30 answer 3, ADR open question 3, `:503–505`).
- **Reactions are out of scope** for the engine (ADR K5, `:107–111`).
- **Tiered thresholds go to T109 and modifier conditions to their first
  consumer** (D32g, D32h). A condition consumer is covered without change,
  because every fold here is a kernel call over the same view (B6).
- **Rolls are not on the wire in v3** (T140, D52 answer 1), and DCs stay
  hidden unless a ruleset marks them public (D30 answer 8, ADR `:515–519`).
  Who may receive odds is therefore a later protocol and host decision, not
  this task's (Out of scope).
- **AI runs outside the tick order** (D30 answer 9, `:520–521`), so an AI
  may call a read-only query like this one. None does yet.
- **One legality source** (T028, ADR-0018, invariant 12).

Decisions this contract makes:
- **D1. The preview of an illegal action is that action's rejection.** It
  calls `validate_action` and returns its error unchanged
  (`PreviewError::Rejected`). Odds for an action the actor cannot take now
  would need a second, looser legality predicate, which invariant 12
  forbids. In turn-based play only the active combatant gets odds; in
  real-time play any live combatant does. This is what players can be shown,
  so it is question 1.
- **D2. Reuse the plan; repeat nothing the kernel does.** The pipeline,
  views, synthetic bonus modifiers and `Against` come from `action_plan`.
  The non-opposed defence value is `resolve`'s own `against_value`, from one
  call whose actor side is `RollSpec::None` (B4): no draw, and the kernel's
  own `Dc` and `Stat` preflight. Each raw total is folded by
  `query_numeric`, and each margin is decided by `OutcomeTable::evaluate`.
  *Rejected:* re-deriving the defence fold in `crpg-sim`, which is a second
  copy of `resolution.rs:563–611` that could drift.
- **D3. Counts, not fractions.** An outcome's probability is
  `count / attack_space`; a damage value's probability given that outcome is
  `n / damage_space`. All are `u64`. Attack and damage counts are kept
  apart, because a joint denominator would overflow sooner and the damage
  draw happens only for outcomes that have dice. No `f32`/`f64` and no
  `Fx16_16`: nothing in U9 or the follow-on row allows a rounded value.
- **D4. One bound, `MAX_PREVIEW_COST` = 4,096, plus `u64` exactness.** A
  dice expression is either *summed* (selection `All` and no natural face:
  a convolution, cost `count × (sides − 1) + 1`, the number of possible
  totals) or *enumerated* (anything else: every face tuple, cost
  `sides^count`). Each expression's cost must be at most 4,096, and its
  tuple count `sides^count` must fit in `u64`; the attack's two tuple counts
  must also multiply within `u64`. Over the bound is an error naming the
  part (B3), never an approximation. What it admits: `1d20`, `2d20kh1`,
  `1d100`, `4d6dl1` (1,296), `1d4096`, damage up to `24d6`; what it refuses:
  `3d20kh1` (8,000), `1d4097`, `25d6` (6^25 > `u64::MAX`). The worst case
  is an `Opposed` pair of 4,096 rows each, about 16.8 million margin
  steps. The value is question 2.
- **D5. Rows are the table's outcomes, in table order, zero counts
  included.** A UI can show "0 %" and keep a stable list. Rows are each
  distinct outcome of the bands in authored order, then each `Override`
  outcome of the natural rules in rule order (B9). An outcome that has a
  damage entry but no row is never evaluated.
- **D6. Damage is the distribution per outcome, not a kill chance.** A kill
  chance needs the target's health, which a viewer may not be granted (U7);
  it is derivable by a consumer that holds both. Effects attached on success
  are not previewed (Out of scope).
- **D7. One new module, `preview.rs`**, reading `combat.rs` through
  `pub(crate)` items, so the 5,443-line `combat.rs` gains no logic and
  execution cannot change.
- **D8. No serde on the new types.** Nothing serializes a preview yet; the
  wire shape belongs to the protocol task that first carries it, so this task
  pins no bytes.
- **D9. No consumer is built.** The host answering a client's preview
  request, the protocol message, an AI scorer and a CLI "odds" command are
  all later tasks (Out of scope). T143 ships the query and its proof.

Imports include, from `crpg_core` (`DeterministicRng`, `EntityId`, `Ulid`),
`crpg_rules` (`resolve`, `Against`, `DiceExpr`, `DiceSelection`,
`ModifierTarget`, `NaturalEffect`, `Outcome`, `QueryContext`,

```rust
/// The largest cost of one dice expression a preview evaluates (T143).
///
/// A summed expression (selection `All`, no natural face) costs
/// `count × (sides − 1) + 1`; any other costs `sides^count`.
pub const MAX_PREVIEW_COST: u64 = 4096;

/// The exact odds of one candidate `UseAbility`, read-only (T143,
/// ADR-0029 U9).
///
/// Every face tuple of the ability's dice (and of an `Opposed` defence's
/// dice) is equally likely; `attack_space` counts them. An outcome's
/// probability is `count / attack_space`.
#[derive(Debug, Clone, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub struct ActionPreview {
    /// Equally likely attack draws: the actor's face tuples times the
    /// opposing face tuples (one when the defence is not `Opposed`).
    pub attack_space: u64,
    /// One row per distinct outcome of the ability's table, in table order;
    /// the counts sum to `attack_space`.
    pub outcomes: Vec<OutcomeOdds>,
}

/// One outcome's share of the attack draws and the damage it deals.
#[derive(Debug, Clone, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub struct OutcomeOdds {
    /// The outcome label.
    pub outcome: Outcome,
    /// Attack draws that decide this outcome; zero when none does.
    pub count: u64,
    /// Of `count`, the draws in which a natural-face rule matched the
    /// actor's natural face.
    pub natural: u64,
    /// Equally likely damage draws for this outcome: the face tuples of its
    /// damage dice, or one when it has none.
    pub damage_space: u64,
    /// `(damage, draws)` pairs, ascending by damage, draws nonzero, summing
    /// to `damage_space`. A damage's probability given this outcome is
    /// `draws / damage_space`.
    pub damage: Vec<(u32, u64)>,
}

/// The dice expression whose exact distribution exceeds the bound.
#[derive(Debug, Clone, Copy, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub enum PreviewPart {
    /// The ability's own dice.
    Actor,
    /// An `Opposed` defence's dice.
    Opposing,
    /// The actor's and opposing tuple counts together.
    Attack,
    /// The damage dice of this outcome's row.
    Damage {
        /// The row's outcome.
        outcome: Outcome,
    },
}

/// Why a preview produced no odds.
#[derive(Debug, Clone, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub enum PreviewError {
    /// The candidate fails [`validate_action`]; this is its error, unchanged.
    Rejected(CombatError),
    /// The exact distribution would exceed [`MAX_PREVIEW_COST`] or `u64`.
    TooLarge {
        /// The first part over the bound, in check order.
        part: PreviewPart,
    },
}

/// Returns the exact outcome and damage distribution of
/// `UseAbility { actor, ability, target }` against the current state,
/// without mutating `world` or drawing from its RNG (T143, ADR-0029 U9).
pub fn preview_action(
    world: &World,
    actor: EntityId,
    ability: Ulid,
    target: EntityId,
) -> Result<ActionPreview, PreviewError>;
```

## Behaviour
Terms. For a `DiceExpr` `e` with `count` n, `sides` s, `selection` and
`offset`: `tuples(e)` is `s^n` computed with checked `u64` arithmetic
(`None` on overflow). A *face tuple* is `(f_0, …, f_{n−1})` with each
`f_i` in `1..=s`, in draw order. `e` is *summed* when its selection is
`All` and no natural face is read from it; otherwise it is *enumerated*.
The *actor expression* is the ability's `dice`, read with a natural face
exactly when `natural_die` is `Some`. The *opposing expression* is an
`Opposed` defence's `dice`, never read with a natural face. A row's *damage
expression* is the first `damage_dice` entry for the row's outcome, if any.

1. **B1 (validation first).** `preview_action` first calls
   `validate_action(world, &CombatAction::UseAbility { actor, ability,
   target })`. An `Err(e)` returns `Err(PreviewError::Rejected(e))` with `e`
   unchanged, before any bound check, so a rejected candidate never reports
   `TooLarge`. Validation covers turn order (TB), the terminal gate (RT),
   self-targets, affordability, T137's stat evaluation stage and range.
2. **B2 (cost).** `cost(e)` is `n × (s − 1) + 1` for a summed expression
   and `tuples(e)` for an enumerated one. An expression *passes* when
   `tuples(e)` is `Some` and `cost(e) ≤ MAX_PREVIEW_COST`. `n × (s − 1) + 1`
   is at most 1,024 × 999,999 + 1 = 1,023,998,977, so it never overflows
   `u64`.
3. **B3 (bound order).** After B1 and before any query, `resolve` call or
   distribution, the checks run in this order; the first failure returns
   `Err(TooLarge { part })`:
   1. the actor expression passes, else `Actor`;
   2. for an `Opposed` defence, the opposing expression passes, else
      `Opposing`;
   3. `tuples(actor) × tuples(opposing)` (the second factor 1 without
      `Opposed`) fits `u64`, else `Attack`;
   4. for each row in B9 order that has a damage expression, it passes, else
      `Damage { outcome }` of that row.
   A row with count 0 is still checked; an outcome without a row is not
   (B9).
4. **B4 (plan and defence value).** The entry is `ability_entry(definition,
   ability)` and the plan is `action_plan(world,
   world.interners().clone(), entry, actor, target)`, unwrapped with `expect`:
   after B1 it cannot fail (when `derived` is nonempty `validate_action` built
   the same plan; otherwise no query can fail). The `TargetStat` world intern
   of `resolve_use` (`combat.rs:3911–3913`) is not repeated. The views are
   `resolve_use`'s (`:3941–3980`): when `actor == target`, one view
   (`actor`, `actor_stats`, `actor_tags`, `actor_mods`) serves both sides;
   otherwise the actor view and the target view (`target`, `target_stats`,
   `target_tags`, `target_mods`). When the plan's `against` is not
   `Opposed`, the defence value is the `against_value` of one call
   `resolve(&request, &context, &mut DeterministicRng::from_seed(0))`, with
   `context` as in `resolve_use` and `request = ResolutionRequest { roll:
   RollRequest { actor, roll: RollSpec::None, bonus_target: roll_tag,
   natural_die: None, stream: String::new(), tags: TagSet::new() }, target:
   Some(target), against: plan.against, outcome_table:
   entry.outcome_table.id() }`, unwrapped with `expect`. That call draws
   nothing and its RNG is discarded.
5. **B5 (expression distribution).** The distribution of `e` maps `(raw,
   natural)` to the number of face tuples that give it:
   - `raw` = `clamp(i64(offset) + Σ kept f_i, i32::MIN, i32::MAX)`, where the
     kept faces are all of them for `All`, the k largest for
     `KeepHighest(k)`, the k smallest for `KeepLowest(k)`, all but the d
     largest for `DropHighest(d)`, and all but the d smallest for
     `DropLowest(d)` (which equal faces are kept does not change the sum);
   - `natural` = `Some(f_k)` when the face is read with `natural_die =
     Some(k)`, even if `f_k` is not kept; otherwise `None`.
   A summed expression is computed by convolution, an enumerated one by
   visiting every tuple; for the same expression read without a natural face
   both give the same map. The counts sum to `tuples(e)`.
6. **B6 (folds).** Each distinct actor `raw` folds to `T_a` =
   `pipeline.query_numeric(actor, ModifierTarget::Roll(roll_tag), raw,
   &actor_view)`'s value. With `Opposed`, each distinct opposing `raw`
   folds to `T_o` = `pipeline.query_numeric(target,
   ModifierTarget::Roll(roll_tag), raw, &target_view)`'s value (the one view when self-targeted). Both unwrap
   with `expect`. The views' tags are used as they are: `resolve` unions
   them with the request's tags, which combat leaves empty
   (`combat.rs:3935`, `:3387`). Without `Opposed` there is one opposing
   entry: `T_o` = the B4 defence value, with count 1.
7. **B7 (decision).** For every actor entry `((raw_a, natural), c_a)` and
   every opposing entry `(raw_o, c_o)`: `margin = i64(T_a) − i64(T_o)`,
   `d = entry.outcome_table.evaluate(margin, natural)`. The row of
   `d.outcome` gains `c_a × c_o` in `count`, and in `natural` too when
   `d.natural_rule_index` is `Some`. The opposing face never reaches
   `evaluate`. No product or sum overflows, because the counts sum to
   `attack_space` (B3 step 3).
8. **B8 (damage).** For each row with outcome `o`: `flat` is the amount of
   the first `damage` entry for `o`, or 0. With a damage expression, every
   `(t, n)` of its distribution (read without a natural face) adds `n` to
   the damage `clamp(i64(flat) + i64(t), 0, u32::MAX)`, and `damage_space`
   is its `tuples`. Without one, `damage_space` is 1 and `damage` is
   `[(flat, 1)]`. `damage` is ascending by damage, one pair per value, no
   zero count.
9. **B9 (rows).** The rows are the distinct outcomes of the table: each
   band's outcome in authored band order, then each natural rule's
   `Override` outcome in authored rule order, each outcome once at its first
   appearance (`Shift` rules add none). Every row is present even with
   `count` 0. `attack_space` is `tuples(actor) × tuples(opposing)`, and the
   rows' `count`s sum to it.
10. **B10 (determinism).** The result is a function of the world's state
    other than its RNG: two calls on one world are equal, and two worlds that
    differ only in their RNG give equal results.
11. **B11 (read-only).** `preview_action` takes `&World` and returns, Ok or
    Err, with the world equal to a clone taken before the call (`World:
    PartialEq`), the same `serde_json` bytes and the same `state_hash`. It
    adds no interner entry, including the defence stat of a `TargetStat`
    ability that no action has interned yet. It calls no `&mut World`
    function and touches no RNG stream.
12. **B12 (agreement with execution).** For every candidate it accepts, with
    every face drawn uniformly and independently (Reading 7), the preview
    equals what `resolve_use` does on the same state (through
    `perform_action` in turn-based play, or a due order in real-time play):
    - a row's `count` is the number of attack face tuples (actor tuple, then
      opposing tuple, in draw order) that decide its outcome;
    - its `natural` is the number of those whose actor face at `natural_die`
      equals a natural rule's face;
    - for a row with a damage expression, the count paired with damage `v`
      is the number of its face tuples (drawn after the attack) that give
      damage `v`.
    Tests G1–G6 prove it, in turn-based play, on seven worlds.
13. **B13 (modes).** In turn-based play only the active combatant gets odds
    (B1). In real-time play any live combatant does while two or more are
    alive. The odds describe resolution against the current state. A
    real-time order resolves later against the state at its due tick, which
    may differ; the preview says nothing about when.
14. **B14 (neutrality).** No production code names a ruleset, stat, tag, die
    size, outcome or modifier type. The only new string literals are the
    Display texts under Errors and `expect` messages.

## Out of scope and hand-off
Each item names who owns it.
- **Carrying odds to a player.** Protocol v3 has no preview message (T140,
  D52 answer 1). A later protocol version adds the request and reply, and
  its host task answers it by calling `preview_action`. That pair also
  decides who may receive odds: odds narrow down a hidden DC, and DCs stay
  hidden unless a ruleset marks them public (D30 answer 8), so it is an
  H-filter decision there. The client draws them (T051's successor), when a
  ruleset's `show_odds` asks for it. The manager assigns these IDs; T141 does
  not answer previews.
- **AI and tools.** An AI scorer (the first `crpg-ai` task that rates
  actions) and any `crpgc` "odds" command are later consumers. None is
  built here (D9).
- **Reactions and interrupts:** out of the engine's scope (ADR K5); revisit
  with hook dispatch (T053).
- **Movement:** an action never moves anyone; range is only a validation
  stage (ADR-0036 X2), inherited through B1.
- **Real-time timing:** when a standing order resolves, and the state it
  meets then, is T138's; the preview describes resolution now (B13).
- **What happens after the hit:** effect attachment and its later folds,
  death, and the turn advance. Whether an effect attaches follows from the
  `Success`/`CriticalSuccess` rows; a kill chance needs health the viewer may
  not hold (D6). A later task may add either, with its own consumer.
- **New draws.** T134's counting modes (deferred, D33), exploding dice and
  glitch rules (deferred, D30 answer 3) and T109's tiered thresholds (D32g)
  all change how a resolution draws or decides. Whichever task first makes
  one executable extends B5, B7 or B8 and the G sweep in the same task;
  invariant 15 (Definition of done) makes that a rule. Modifier conditions
  (D32h) need nothing: the folds are kernel calls over the same views.
- **Initiative, costs and spending:** start-time draws (T137 B17) and pool
  spending are not odds; affordability is checked through B1.
- **Caching and incremental updates:** none. Each call recomputes.

## Questions for the user
1. **When can a player see odds?** "Odds" here means the chance that an
   attack hits, crits or misses, and how much damage it might do. The engine
   already has one rule deciding whether an action is allowed right now (it is
   your turn, you can pay for it, the target is in range). (a) Show odds only
   for actions that rule allows right now: in turn-based play, only on your
   own turn; in real-time play, at any time. (b) Also show odds for actions
   you could not take right now (not your turn, cannot pay), for planning.
   That needs a second, looser allowed-or-not rule, and two such rules
   drift apart, which is why the engine forbids a second one today.
   *Recommend (a).* A player chooses actions on their own turn, where the
   odds are wanted, and (b) can be added later as its own decision.
2. **How large a dice roll can the odds cover?** The odds are exact, so the
   engine counts every way the dice can land, and very large rolls take too
   long to count. The limit is 4,096 "cost units" per roll: a plain sum of
   dice costs the number of totals it can reach, and a keep-highest,
   keep-lowest or natural-face roll costs every way the dice can land. (a)
   4,096: covers d20, d100, advantage (2d20 keep highest), 4d6 drop lowest
   (1,296), any single die up to d4,096, and damage up to 24d6; refuses 3d20
   keep highest (8,000) and 25d6 damage (too many ways to count in a 64-bit
   number). The slowest case is an opposed d4,096 against d4,096, about 17
   million steps. (b) 65,536, which also covers 3d20 keep highest, but
   needs a smarter counting method for opposed rolls (about 150 more lines).
   (c) 1,024, which is faster but refuses 4d6 drop lowest. Over the limit,
   the engine reports "too large to preview" and never shows a rounded guess.
   *Recommend (a):* it covers every roll in the shipped rulesets and the
   usual d20 and percentile systems, and raising it later changes no saved
   game or protocol.
