## Task
ADR-0029 U9 (`docs/adr/0029-kernel-range-and-time-model.md:397–399`):
"`show_odds` displays the outcome distribution from a server-side read-only
preview query (Follow-on 10), never client math". Follow-on 10 (`:489`,
`docs/IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md:217`) makes it this task: "`crpg-sim`, read-only
action preview: exact integer outcome distribution, bounded (U9)".

Build `preview_action(world, actor, ability, target, rules)`. For one
candidate `UseAbility`, it returns three things for every outcome of the
ability's table:
- how many of the equally likely attack draws decide that outcome;
- how many of those a natural-face rule decided;
- the exact distribution of the damage that outcome deals.

Every probability is an exact count, of any size, over a stated total. The
function takes `&World`, so it mutates nothing and draws nothing from the
world's RNG.

The ruleset decides two things through a new optional `preview` field
(T143a):
- when the server answers a preview: never, only for an action the actor
  may take now, or also for planning out of turn;
- how much work one preview may take, up to an engine ceiling.

A preview over its ruleset's limit is refused with the cost it would have
needed. It is never approximated.

The preview is the real resolution counted, not a second rules model. It
admits the action through the one legality source, builds the same
`ActionPlan` execution uses (`crates/crpg-sim/src/combat.rs:3229`), takes the
defence value from the kernel's own `resolve`, and folds every possible roll
through `ModifierPipeline::query_numeric` and `OutcomeTable::evaluate`. The
only arithmetic it repeats is the dice themselves and the damage clamp.
Seed-sweep tests compare it with `perform_action` draw for draw (G1–G7).

## Readings
What the code does today, each checked at `095ed20`.

1. **Execution path.** An accepted turn-based `UseAbility` runs
   `resolve_use` (`crates/crpg-sim/src/combat.rs:3899`). It builds the
   `ActionPlan` (`:3914–3916`), resolves through the kernel's `resolve` on
   `combat.roll` (`:3955`, `:3978`), then draws the damage dice with
   `damage_for` (`:3981`, `:4034–4050`). Real-time due orders run the same
   `resolve_use` from `execution_stage` (`:4520`, `:4580`), so the two
   modes resolve alike (T138 changes when, not how).
2. **The plan.** `action_plan` (`:3229–3423`) builds the pipeline, both
   views (stats, tags, modifiers), the bonus-stat synthetic modifiers and the
   `Against` for all five defences, from a scratch interner. It reads the
   world and writes nothing.
3. **Its one world write is outside the plan.** For a `TargetStat` defence,
   `resolve_use` interns the stat into the world interner before cloning it
   (`:3911–3913`, T137 B10). Inside the plan the stat is always interned in
   the scratch clone too, because it is a usable stat (T137 B6). A plan built
   without that write therefore gives the same values (B12).
4. **Legality.** `validate_action` (`:3448`) is the one legality source
   (`crates/crpg-sim/AGENTS.md:269`, invariant 12): "Never re-check admission
   in a second predicate or validate by executing on a clone". For
   `UseAbility` it checks, in order:
   1. participants, then absent and dead (`:3517–3540`);
   2. the turn stage (turn-based: `active != Some(actor)`; real-time: the
      terminal gate "fewer than two alive", `:3541–3558`);
   3. `UnknownAbility` and `SelfTarget` (`:3559–3564`);
   4. affordability (`:3565–3610`);
   5. T137's stat evaluation stage (`:3611–3615`);
   6. range.

   A finished encounter has `active == None` (`set_terminal`, `:4306–4313`).
   `rt_alive` counts the combatants that are not dead (`:4317–4323`).
5. **Resolution order.** `resolve` (`crates/crpg-rules/src/resolution.rs:480`)
   evaluates a non-opposed defence before any draw (`:563`). It draws the
   actor's dice and then the opposing dice on one staged RNG. It folds each
   raw total through `query_numeric(…, ModifierTarget::Roll(…), raw, view)`
   and decides with `outcome_table.evaluate(margin, actor_natural)`
   (`:703–704`). A side with `RollSpec::None` has raw total zero and touches
   no stream (`:273`). A resolution that draws nothing leaves the RNG
   unchanged (`:705–707`).
6. **Natural faces.** `RollRequest.natural_die` selects a raw draw index,
   "even when that die is later dropped" (`resolution.rs:288–293`). The
   opposing roll of `Opposed` has `natural_die: None` (`combat.rs:3385`, T137
   B13). `OutcomeTable::evaluate` (`resolution.rs:197–222`) applies at most
   one rule, the one whose face equals the natural face. A face that matches
   no rule decides exactly as no natural face does: the band's outcome, with
   `natural_rule_index: None`.
7. **Dice.** `DiceExpr::evaluate` (`crates/crpg-rules/src/dice.rs:142–170`)
   draws `count` faces uniformly on `1..=sides` and keeps them by
   `selection` (`:197–249`). It clamps the kept sum plus offset to `i32`
   once (`:168`). Each face is drawn by `gen_range_u32`, which redraws below
   a threshold to avoid modulo bias (`crates/crpg-core/src/rng.rs:74–84`).
   Every face tuple is therefore equally likely, and the dice are
   exchangeable, so counting tuples is the exact distribution.
8. **Damage.** `damage_for` takes the amount of the first flat entry for the
   outcome, or 0. When there is a damage-dice entry for that outcome, it adds
   the first one's total in `i64` and clamps once into `0..=u32::MAX`. An
   outcome without dice draws nothing (`combat.rs:4034–4050`, T137 B16).
9. **Input bounds.** One expression has at most 1,024 dice of at most
   1,000,000 sides (`crates/crpg-rules/src/lib.rs:66`, `:68`). A table has at
   most 256 bands and 256 natural rules (`:70`, `:72`). The number of face
   tuples, `sides^count`, overflows any fixed-width integer long before
   those limits.
10. **Where ruleset policy can live.** The UI document (`crpg.ui/1`, with
    its per-ability `show_odds`, `tasks/T142.md:253`) is presentation data
    that the server ignores (U1, `docs/adr/0029-kernel-range-and-time-model.md:353–355`).
    The ruleset document is what the server loads. Its optional policy
    fields (`creature_persistence`, `late_join`, `effects_on_exit`,
    `crates/crpg-data/src/combat.rs:402–409`) are plain snake-case enums with
    a hand-written `Deserialize` (`:447–490`). Read-time checks live in
    `validate_combat_local`'s `Ruleset` arm (`:1499–1504`).
11. **The wire.** Protocol v3 carries no roll or preview message (T140
    question 1, answered (a), `tasks/T140.md:1710–1724`). No crate calls
    anything like a preview today.

## Decisions
Decisions already made, which this contract follows:
- **U9 and Follow-on 10** (ADR-0029, accepted D30c): odds come from a
  server-side, read-only query, as an exact integer distribution, bounded;
  never client math.
- **T137's hand-off** (`tasks/T137.md:821–822`): model `Opposed` and
  damage-dice draws, and the B15 stage. B5, B7 and B9 do, and admission
  runs B15 in both scopes.
- **U1**: the server ignores the UI document. So the server-side policy
  cannot be `show_odds`; it is a ruleset field (D1).
- **The current-tag rule** (D32c): an optional field whose absence keeps
  the earlier behaviour joins `crpg.ruleset/2` with no version bump.
- **T134 stays deferred** (D33). Exploding dice and glitch rules are
  deferred too (D30 answer 3). Reactions are out of scope (ADR K5,
  `:107–111`). Tiered thresholds go to T109 and modifier conditions to their
  first consumer (D32g, D32h).
- **Rolls are not on the wire in v3** (D52 answer 1). DCs stay hidden unless
  a ruleset marks them public (D30 answer 8).
- **AI runs outside the tick order** (D30 answer 9), so an AI may call this
  query. None does yet.
- **One legality source** (T028, ADR-0018, invariant 12).
- **Routing (user, 2026-11-13, while only Claude workers run):** a package
  that is not S-set gets no separate blind two-keys writer. It gets one Claude
  reviewer mutant run on the implementer's suite, then one fix round.

Flags against accepted decisions (none is a conflict; each is a reading
the user can overrule):
- **Follow-on 10 names one crate.** The ruleset-defined policy needs a
  `crpg-data` field, so T143 gains a companion package, T143a. That keeps
  one crate per package.
- **Two switches.** The ruleset's `preview.when` decides whether the server
  answers. The UI document's per-ability `show_odds` decides whether the
  client asks and shows. A UI that asks while the ruleset says `never`
  shows nothing. A later T142 follow-up may add a validation warning for
  that pair (Out of scope).
- **D30 answer 8.** Odds narrow down a hidden DC. A ruleset that sets
  `when` to anything but `never` has chosen to show that much. Absence means
  `never` (D3, question 1), so no existing ruleset starts leaking.
- **Invariant 12.** The one legality source gains one parameter, the
  admission scope (B2). It is still one function. Every stage except the
  turn and affordability stages applies to both scopes, and a new stage
  added there reaches both.

Decisions this contract makes:
- **D1. The ruleset owns the policy.** `Ruleset.preview: Option<PreviewWire>`
  holds `when` (`never`, `actionable` or `planning`) and an optional
  `max_cost`. The sim's `PreviewRules` is its mapped form. `preview_action`
  takes `&PreviewRules` as an argument, so the sim carries no new hashed
  state. A host task calls it with the rules mapped from the ruleset it
  loaded, and answers nothing when the mapping is `None` (`never`).
- **D2. Planning keeps one legality source.** Under `planning`, admission
  skips exactly two stages:
  - the turn stage, replaced by "fewer than two alive" (`OutOfTurn { actor,
    active: None }`), the same terminal gate real-time play uses;
  - the affordability stage, because odds do not depend on costs.

  Everything else runs, in the same order: participants, death, unknown
  ability, self-target, the stat evaluation stage and range. `validate_action`
  is the `actionable` scope and behaves exactly as today. *Rejected:*
  validating a modified clone ("as if it were your turn"), which invariant 12
  forbids and which could not refill pools faithfully anyway.
- **D3. Absent means `never`.** Absence of `preview` maps to `None`: no
  preview. This keeps the current-tag rule (absence is the old behaviour),
  matches `show_odds` defaulting to false and DCs hidden by default, and is
  question 1.
- **D4. Exact counts of any size.** `PreviewCount` is an unsigned integer
  of any width. An outcome's probability is `count / attack_space`, and a
  damage value's probability given its outcome is `n / damage_space`. No
  float, no `Fx16_16` and no rounding. *Rejected:* `u64` or `u128` counts,
  which would refuse `25d6` and `50d6` on representation alone, against the
  user's "as large as possible".
- **D5. Counting is polynomial in dice and sides.** No face tuple is
  enumerated:
  - plain sums by convolution over the reachable totals;
  - keep and drop by a dynamic program over how many dice show each face,
    with exact binomial counts;
  - natural faces by holding the selected die at each natural-rule face, so
    the rest is the same problem with one die fewer or one die fixed.

  Each algorithm has a pinned cost formula (B3).
- **D6. One cost budget per preview: the ruleset's `max_cost`, capped by the
  engine ceiling `MAX_PREVIEW_COST` = 16,777,216.** The cost of a preview is
  the sum of its parts (B3), measured before any work. It is in work units
  that include the width of the counts. The effective limit is
  `min(rules.max_cost, MAX_PREVIEW_COST)`, so no ruleset can stall the host
  beyond the ceiling.
  - The ceiling is about 0.1 s of counting in a release build. It fits sums
    up to about `500d6`, `10d10kh3` (37,510), any keep-highest of two dice
    up to `2d1364kh1`, and opposed `1d1000` against `1d1000`.
  - Data rejects a declared `max_cost` outside `1..=MAX_PREVIEW_COST`.
  - An absent `max_cost` means the ceiling.
  - Over the limit is `TooLarge`, naming the first part that crosses it
    together with the full cost and the limit, so an author can tune
    `max_cost`.
  - The constant lives in `crpg-data`, the lower crate, and `crpg-sim`
    re-exports it, so the two cannot disagree.
- **D7. Rows are the table's outcomes, in table order, zero counts
  included** (B10). An outcome that has a damage entry but no row is never
  evaluated or charged.
- **D8. Damage is the distribution per outcome, not a kill chance.** A kill
  chance needs the target's health, which a viewer may not be granted (U7).
  Effects attached on success are not previewed.
- **D9. No serde on the sim types, and no consumer built.** The wire shape
  belongs to the protocol task that first carries odds. The host handler,
  HUD, AI and CLI uses are later tasks.

#[derive(Debug, Clone, Copy, PartialEq, Eq)]

    /// Exactly `validate_action`.
    Actionable,
    /// `validate_action` without the turn and affordability stages.
    Planning,
}

/// A ruleset's preview policy as the sim applies it.
#[derive(Debug, Clone, Copy, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub struct PreviewRules {
    /// Which admission runs.
    pub scope: PreviewScope,
    /// The ruleset's cost limit; the effective limit is
    /// `min(max_cost, MAX_PREVIEW_COST)`.
    pub max_cost: u64,
}

impl PreviewRules {
    /// Maps a ruleset's field: `None` for absent or `never` (no preview is

    /// `MAX_PREVIEW_COST`.

/// The exact odds of one candidate `UseAbility`, read-only.
#[derive(Debug, Clone, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub struct ActionPreview {
    /// Equally likely attack draws: the actor's face tuples times the
    /// opposing face tuples (one factor of 1 without `Opposed`).
    pub attack_space: PreviewCount,
    /// One row per distinct outcome of the table, in table order; the
    /// counts sum to `attack_space`.
    pub outcomes: Vec<OutcomeOdds>,
}

#[derive(Debug, Clone, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub struct OutcomeOdds {
    /// The outcome label.
    pub outcome: Outcome,
    /// Attack draws that decide this outcome; zero when none does.
    pub count: PreviewCount,
    /// Of `count`, the draws in which a natural-face rule matched.
    pub natural: PreviewCount,
    /// Equally likely damage draws: the face tuples of the outcome's damage
    /// dice, or one when it has none.
    pub damage_space: PreviewCount,
    /// `(damage, draws)`, ascending by damage, draws nonzero, summing to
    /// `damage_space`.
    pub damage: Vec<(u32, PreviewCount)>,
}

/// The part of a preview whose cost crossed the limit.
#[derive(Debug, Clone, Copy, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub enum PreviewPart {
    /// The ability's dice.
    Actor,
    /// An `Opposed` defence's dice.

## Behaviour
Terms. For a `DiceExpr` `e`: `n` = count, `s` = sides, `o` = offset.
- `bitlen(s)` is the number of binary digits of `s` (`32 −
  s.leading_zeros()`; `bitlen(1)` = 1, `bitlen(6)` = 3, `bitlen(20)` = 5).
- `T(i)` = `i × (s − 1) + 1`, the number of sums of `i` dice (`T(0)` = 1).
- `k` is the kept count: `n` for `All`, `k` for `KeepHighest(k)` and
  `KeepLowest(k)`, and `n − d` for `DropHighest(d)` and `DropLowest(d)`.
- `r` is the number of distinct natural-rule faces in `1..=s` when a
  natural face is read from `e`, and 0 otherwise.
- The *actor expression* is the ability's `dice`, read with the natural
  face `natural_die`. The *opposing expression* is an `Opposed` defence's
  `dice`, never read with a natural face. A row's *damage expression* is the
  first `damage_dice` entry for the row's outcome, if any, never read with
  a natural face.

Items are marked [a], [b] or [c] by package.

1. **B1 (admission first) [c].** `preview_action` first calls `admit(world,
   &CombatAction::UseAbility { actor, ability, target }, scope)`, with
   `Execute` for `Actionable` and `Plan` for `Planning`. An `Err(e)` returns
   `Err(PreviewError::Rejected(e))` unchanged, before any cost check.
2. **B2 (the planning scope) [c].** `admit` with `Plan` differs from
   `Execute` in exactly two places, both for `UseAbility`:
   - **Turn stage.** In both modes it becomes: if `rt_alive(world) < 2`,
     `Err(OutOfTurn { actor, active: None })`, else continue.
   - **Affordability.** Both affordability loops (template pools, then
     unknown pools) are skipped.

   Every other stage runs in its existing order. `Execute` is today's
   `validate_action` byte for byte: same checks, order, errors and
   precedence. `perform_action`, `legal_actions` and real-time admission
   keep calling `validate_action`.
3. **B3 (costs) [b, c].** With `limbs(e)` = `⌈n × bitlen(s) ÷ 64⌉`:
   - **Summed** (selection `All`): `steps(e)` = `Σ_{i=1..n} T(i)` = `n + (s −
     1) × n × (n + 1) ÷ 2`, plus `2 × r × T(n − 1)` when `r > 0`.
     `rows(e)` = `T(n)`, plus `r × T(n − 1)` when `r > 0`.
   - **Kept** (any other selection): `steps(e)` = `s × (n + 1)² × (k × s +
     1)`, times `(2r + 1)` when `r > 0`. `rows(e)` = `k × (s − 1) + 1`,
     times `(r + 1)` when `r > 0`.
   - `cost(e)` = `steps(e) × limbs(e)`, in checked `u64` (`None` on
     overflow).

   The preview's cost is the sum, in this order, of:
   1. **actor**: `cost(actor expression)`;
   2. **opposing**: `cost(opposing expression)`, or 0 without `Opposed`;
   3. **attack**: `rows(actor) × rows(opposing) × limbs(actor) ×
      limbs(opposing)`, where the opposing factors are 1 and 1 without
      `Opposed`;
   4. **damage**: for each row in B10 order that has a damage expression,
      its cost.

   Every sum and product is checked. These formulas are the contract; an
   implementation's real work must stay proportional to them.
4. **B4 (the limit) [c].** After B1 and before any query, `resolve` call or
   distribution, the effective limit is `limit = min(rules.max_cost,
   MAX_PREVIEW_COST)`. The parts are added in B3's order. The first part
   whose running total exceeds `limit`, or overflows, is `part`. The result
   is then `Err(TooLarge { part, cost, limit })`, where `cost` is the full
   sum of all parts (`None` when any step overflows). A row with count 0 is
   still charged; an outcome without a row is not.
5. **B5 (plan and defence value) [c].** The entry is `ability_entry`. The
   plan is `action_plan(world, world.interners().clone(), entry, actor,
   target)`, unwrapped with `expect`: after B1 it cannot fail, in either
   scope, because both run the stat evaluation stage. The `TargetStat` world
   intern of `resolve_use` (`combat.rs:3911–3913`) is not repeated.

   The views are `resolve_use`'s (`:3941–3980`). When `actor == target`,
   one view serves both sides; otherwise there is an actor view and a
   target view.

   When `against` is not `Opposed`, the defence value is the
   `against_value` of one call `resolve(&request, &context, &mut
   DeterministicRng::from_seed(0))`, unwrapped with `expect`. `context` is
   `resolve_use`'s, and `request` is
   `ResolutionRequest { roll: RollRequest { actor, roll: RollSpec::None,
   bonus_target: roll_tag, natural_die: None, stream: String::new(), tags:
   TagSet::new() }, target: Some(target), against: plan.against,
   outcome_table: entry.outcome_table.id() }`. That call draws nothing.
6. **B6 (distributions) [b].** `distribution(e, natural_die, rule_faces)`
   maps `(raw, tag)` to the number of face tuples that give it.
   - **Raw.** `raw` = `clamp(i64(o) + Σ kept faces, i32::MIN, i32::MAX)`.
     The kept faces are all of them for `All`, the k largest for
     `KeepHighest`/`DropLowest`, and the k smallest for
     `KeepLowest`/`DropHighest`.
   - **Tag.** `tag` = `Some(f)` when `natural_die = Some(j)`, `r > 0`, and the
     face `f` of die `j` is in `rule_faces`, whether or not that die is kept;
     otherwise `None`.
   - **Sums.** The counts sum to `tuples(e)` = `s^n`. The map equals
     enumerating every tuple.
   - **Algorithms.** Required: they define B3's costs.
     - Summed: repeated convolution of one die over the reachable sums.
     - Kept: a dynamic program over face values `s` down to 1 that chooses
       how many dice show each value, weighting by binomial counts. Its state
       is the number of dice placed and the sum kept so far. `KeepLowest`
       mirrors faces (`f → s + 1 − f`). Binomials come from the
       multiplicative recurrence (`mul_small`, `div_small_exact`), with no
       table.
     - Natural, `r > 0`: for each rule face `f`, the same computation with
       one die fixed at `f`. Then the untagged part is the full distribution
       minus the tagged ones.

   The index `j` does not change counts, because dice are exchangeable
   (Reading 7).
7. **B7 (folds) [c].** Each distinct actor `raw` folds to `T_a` =
   `pipeline.query_numeric(actor, ModifierTarget::Roll(roll_tag), raw,
   &actor_view)`'s value. With `Opposed`, each distinct opposing `raw` folds
   to `T_o` = `pipeline.query_numeric(target, ModifierTarget::Roll(roll_tag),
   raw, &target_view)`'s value, using the one view when self-targeted. Both
   unwrap with `expect`. The views' tags are used as they are, because
   `resolve` unions them with the request's tags, which combat leaves empty
   (`combat.rs:3935`, `:3387`). Without `Opposed` there is one opposing
   entry: `T_o` = B5's defence value, with count 1.
8. **B8 (decision) [c].** For every actor entry `((raw_a, tag), c_a)` and
   opposing entry `(raw_o, c_o)`, let `margin` = `i64(T_a) − i64(T_o)` and
   `d` = `entry.outcome_table.evaluate(margin, tag)`. The row of `d.outcome`
   gains `c_a × c_o` in `count`, and in `natural` too when
   `d.natural_rule_index` is `Some`. Passing `tag` in place of the raw face
   decides identically (Reading 6). `evaluate` runs at most once per
   distinct `(margin, tag)`.
9. **B9 (damage) [c].** For a row with outcome `o`, `flat` is the amount of
   the first `damage` entry for `o`, or 0.
   - With a damage expression: every `(t, n)` of its distribution adds `n`
     to the damage `clamp(i64(flat) + i64(t), 0, u32::MAX)`, and
     `damage_space` is its `tuples`.
   - Without one: `damage_space` is 1 and `damage` is `[(flat, 1)]`.

   `damage` is ascending, with one pair per value and no zero count.
10. **B10 (rows) [c].** The rows are the distinct outcomes of the table, each
    at its first appearance: band outcomes in authored order, then
    `Override` outcomes of the natural rules in rule order. `Shift` rules
    add none. Every row is present, even with `count` 0. `attack_space` =
    `tuples(actor) × tuples(opposing)`, and the counts sum to it.
11. **B11 (determinism) [c].** The result depends on the world's state but
    not its RNG. Two calls are equal, and two worlds that differ only in
    their RNG give equal results.
12. **B12 (read-only) [c].** `preview_action` takes `&World`. Ok or Err, the
    world afterwards equals a clone taken before the call, with the same
    `serde_json` bytes and the same `state_hash`. No interner entry is added,
    including a `TargetStat` stat that no action has interned yet.
13. **B13 (agreement with execution) [c].** For every candidate it accepts,
    the preview equals what `resolve_use` does on the same state:
    - a row's `count` is the number of attack face tuples (actor tuple, then
      opposing tuple, in draw order) that decide its outcome;
    - its `natural` is the number of those whose face at `natural_die` equals
      a rule face;
    - for a row with damage dice, the count paired with `v` is the number of
      its damage tuples that give damage `v`.

    G1–G7 prove it in turn-based play.
14. **B14 (modes) [c].** With `Actionable`, turn-based play gives odds only
    to the active combatant, and real-time play to any live combatant while
    two or more are alive. With `Planning`, both give odds to any live
    combatant while two or more are alive. The odds describe resolution
    against the current state; a real-time order resolves later.
15. **B15 (policy mapping) [c].** `PreviewRules::from_wire(None)` and
    `from_wire(Some(&{ when: Never, .. }))` are `None`. `Actionable` and
    `Planning` map to the same-named scope, with `max_cost` =
    `max_cost.unwrap_or(MAX_PREVIEW_COST)`.
16. **B16 (wire) [a].** `preview` decodes only `{ "when": <name>[,
    "max_cost": <u64>] }`. Absent, it is `None`, and the canonical encoding
    omits it. `max_cost` is checked at read time to be in `1..=16_777_216`.
17. **B17 (neutrality) [a, b, c].** No production code names a ruleset,
    stat, tag, die size, outcome or modifier type. The only new string
    literals are the Display and error texts and `expect` messages.
