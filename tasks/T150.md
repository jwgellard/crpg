## Task
Carry lane 1 over QUIC. Today a connection has one bidirectional stream and
no DATAGRAM frames (`crates/crpg-net-quic/src/lib.rs:6–12`;
`crates/crpg-net-quic/src/tls.rs:44–61`, `:86–91`, `:129–134`), and the host
adapter binds only `/1` (`bind_peer`) and `/2` (`bind_snapshot_peer`)
sessions (`crates/crpg-server/src/quic.rs:650–671`). After T150:

- the vendored quinn-proto carries D23's dedup-window patch as patch `0002`,
  and the T030 impairment matrix has been re-run on native Windows (D23);
- `crpg-net-quic` has a third endpoint kind, `bind_movement`, that speaks
  only `/3`: the `/2` lane stream unchanged, a second client-initiated
  bidirectional stream for lane 1 (intents up, receipts down), and QUIC
  DATAGRAM frames downstream for samples, with the size floor checked at
  connect;
- `crpg-server`'s `QuicHost` binds a `/3` hello with `bind_v4_peer`, moves
  lane-1 frames into `ingest_move`, receipts out of `take_move_receipts`
  onto the lane-1 stream and samples out of `take_samples` as datagrams, and
  traces every one of those calls.

`/1` and `/2` endpoints, connections and sessions keep every byte and rule
they have.

## Readings
Design decisions this contract makes. Each needs approval with the contract
(Questions 1–3 cover Readings 1, 11 and 17).

1. **A new ALPN `/3`, served only by a new endpoint kind.** A second stream
   and DATAGRAM frames change the stream mapping, which ADR-0025 says needs a
   new ALPN and an ADR (`docs/adr/0025-lane0-quic-mapping-and-pinned-trust.md:31–36`,
   `:132–134`; the crate trap, `crates/crpg-net-quic/AGENTS.md:125`). quinn
   holds **one** transport configuration per endpoint, fixed before the ALPN
   is known (`tls::server_config` builds it once per bind,
   `crates/crpg-net-quic/src/server.rs:243–256`), so a `/3` endpoint's
   transport parameters (two bidi streams, a datagram buffer) would also be
   advertised to `/1` and `/2` connections on it. So `/3` gets its own
   endpoint, `QuicServer::bind_movement`, which offers `/3` alone, and
   `bind`, `bind_dual` and `bind_resumable` keep their ALPN lists and
   transport parameters exactly. The suffix numbers the whole connection
   mapping, as `/2` already carries lanes 2 and 3 (`0x81`, `0x91`), so the
   name stays `crpg-lane0/3`. *Rejected:* adding `/3` to `bind_dual`
   (changes the transport parameters every `/1` and `/2` connection on it
   receives, against ADR-0025 item 1 and its addendum item 4, `:25–30`,
   `:174–176`); `crpg-lane1/1` (a second naming scheme for the same
   versioned mapping).
2. **`/3`'s lane stream is `/2`'s, so `framing()` reports `Framing::V2`.**
   `Framing` is the lane-stream header layout (`crates/crpg-net-quic/src/handshake.rs:69–77`).
   A `/3` connection's first stream carries the hello, the welcome and the
   `/2` channel frames exactly, so it is `V2`; the new
   `QuicServer::movement(conn)` / `QuicClient::movement()` say whether lane 1
   and datagrams exist. `Framing::from_alpn(b"crpg-lane0/3")` stays `None`.
   *Rejected:* a `Framing::V3` variant: `crpg-server` matches `Framing`
   exhaustively (`crates/crpg-server/src/quic.rs:650–671`), so T150b alone
   would break the workspace build, and a two-crate part is not allowed
   (`AGENTS.md`, "One task = one crate").
3. **Lane 1 is the second client-initiated bidi stream, 4-byte length
   header, its own queues.** Intents go up and receipts down on it, framed
   by `frame_header`/`parse_frame_header` (`handshake.rs:42–67`), caps
   4,096 up (`MAX_INTENT_FRAME_BYTES`, the cap `decode_intent` enforces,
   `crates/crpg-net/src/movement_v4.rs:228–229`) and 1,024 down
   (`MAX_MOVE_DOWN_BYTES`, `:50`; the largest receipt is 60,
   `tasks/T026p.md:607–609`). Lane 1 has its own sequence domain and never
   touches lane-0 state (ADR-0036 L1, `:389–390`; HM-10,
   `tasks/T026p.md:1025–1026`), so it has its own inbound and outbound
   queues and no order relation to lane 0. The client opens it right after
   reading the welcome; quinn makes a stream visible to the peer only when
   data is written on it (quinn `src/connection.rs:348–355`), so the
   server's second `accept_bi` completes at the client's first lane-1 frame.
   That is enough: a receipt only ever answers an intent that arrived on the
   stream. quinn yields peer streams in stream-ID order, so the first
   `accept_bi` is always the hello stream. *Rejected:* a `/2` channel
   `0xA1` on the first stream (lane 1 would wait in lane 0's one queue
   behind 64 KiB delta frames, and the "one queue per direction" trap,
   `crates/crpg-net-quic/AGENTS.md:156–157`, forbids giving it its own);
   two unidirectional streams (equally sound, but a second stream kind
   beside the bidi pair the reader/writer tasks already serve).
4. **Datagrams go downstream only.** On a movement endpoint the server's
   quinn `datagram_receive_buffer_size` is `Some(0)`: quinn refuses to send
   while the sender's own receive buffer is `None`
   (`third_party/quinn-proto/crate/src/connection/datagrams.rs:29–31`), and
   `Some(0)` advertises `max_datagram_frame_size = 0`
   (`transport_parameters.rs:170–172`), which RFC 9221 §3 defines as "does
   not support DATAGRAM frames". A quinn peer then fails a non-empty send
   with `TooLarge` before anything is sent (`datagrams.rs:32–41`, `:89–95`),
   and an empty one makes the server's quinn close the connection with a
   transport `PROTOCOL_VIOLATION` (`:137–141`). The client's receive buffer
   is `Some(65_536)` (advertised 65,535, the `u16` clamp) and its send
   buffer 0, so it can send no datagram (`:35–38`). *Rejected:* a server
   receive buffer of `None` (the server could not send); one above 0
   (upstream datagrams nothing reads).
5. **The connect-time floor is the server's sendable size.** Right after TLS
   on a `/3` connection the server reads quinn's `max_datagram_size()`; `None`
   or below `MAX_DATAGRAM_BYTES` (1,024) closes with `ProtocolViolation` (2)
   before the hello is read, with no event and `refused_handshakes` + 1 —
   the path a foreign ALPN already takes (`server.rs:1002–1009`). quinn
   computes it as `min(advertised − 9, MTU − (1 + CID + 4 + 16) − 9)`
   (`datagrams.rs:82–96`; `connection/mod.rs:3768–3781`). quinn never lets
   the MTU below 1,200 (`config/transport.rs:199–216`) and its CIDs are 8
   bytes (`cid_generator.rs:49–55`), so the path term is at least
   1,200 − 29 − 9 = 1,162 and only the client's advertisement can fail the
   floor: an advertisement of 1,033 gives exactly 1,024 (accepted), 1,032
   gives 1,023 (refused). *Rejected:* `VersionRefused` (6), a version code
   for a mapping violation; a new close code (codes 0–9 are pinned,
   `crates/crpg-net-quic/src/close.rs:10–32`). **Recorded quinn property,
   not changed here:** a quinn receiver closes the connection on a datagram
   whose length plus 32 exceeds its own receive buffer (`datagrams.rs:137–141`),
   while its advertisement lets a sender use the buffer less 9, so a quinn
   client with a receive buffer of 1,033..=1,055 passes the floor but cannot
   receive a 1,024-byte datagram. This crate's client uses 65,536; a
   third-party client must use at least 1,056 (architecture doc).
6. **A datagram send is a direct, non-blocking hand-off to quinn; there is no
   crate datagram queue on the server.** quinn's `Connection::send_datagram`
   is synchronous: it takes quinn's own lock, queues the datagram and wakes
   the driver, dropping the oldest unsent datagram when its send buffer is
   full (quinn `src/connection.rs:448–466`; `datagrams.rs:42–45`,
   `:154–161`). The sync side already calls quinn outside the state lock in
   `stats` (`server.rs:644–655`). So `try_send_datagram` returning `Ok` means
   "handed to QUIC", which may still drop it, and it never returns
   `QueueFull`. The server's quinn datagram send buffer is 65,536 bytes
   (two worst-case ticks: 27 × (1,024 + 32) = 28,512, `tasks/T037.md:284–294`).
   *Rejected:* a crate queue drained by an I/O task (a second buffer in front
   of quinn's, holding stale samples, with no deterministic `QueueFull` a
   test could pin); `send_datagram_wait` (blocks the caller, against crate
   invariant 2, `crates/crpg-net-quic/AGENTS.md:50–53`).
7. **The client keeps the newest 64 datagrams.** An I/O task reads quinn's
   datagrams into a queue of 64; when it is full, the oldest queued datagram
   is dropped and counted (`Lane1Stats::datagrams_dropped`). A 0-byte
   datagram closes with `ProtocolViolation` (2) and one above 1,024 with
   `FrameTooLarge` (3), the stream rules (`crates/crpg-net-quic/src/queue.rs:724–729`).
   *Rejected:* dropping the newest (a newer sample supersedes an older one,
   L4, `docs/adr/0036-movement-and-navigation.md:402–407`).
8. **Lane-1 queues are fixed per-connection constants, not limits.** Server
   inbound 64 frames / 65,536 bytes, server outbound 128 / 131,072; client
   outbound 64 / 65,536, client inbound 128 / 131,072. Host-wide they are
   bounded by `max_connections` (at most 64, `limits.rs:35`, `:132–137`): 4 MiB
   inbound, 8 MiB outbound on a server. The server's outbound frame cap
   equals `POLICY_EGRESS_FRAMES_PER_PEER` (128), the host's receipt-queue
   bound (`tasks/T037.md:792–795`), and 128 × 1,024 = 131,072, so one full
   take of receipts fits an empty queue (Reading 12). A full inbound lane-1
   queue parks one frame and stops reading, as lane 0 does. *Rejected:*
   counting lane 1 in lane 0's `QueueCaps` (two parked frames per
   connection would contend in one host-wide promotion order); new
   `ServerLimits`/`ClientLimits` fields (`validate` and `v1` changes for
   numbers no operator tunes yet; tightening-only limits can follow).
9. **One connection, one close.** A `Flush` close drains and FINs both
   writers and waits for both acknowledgements inside the same budget and
   reserve (`crates/crpg-net-quic/src/teardown.rs:9–42`; T098g/h's
   `FlushClose::flush_close` is still the only constructor). A close is
   published only after both readers stop; frames either reader received
   stay drainable; the record-lifetime rule applies per lane. Datagrams have
   no flush: unsent ones die with `CONNECTION_CLOSE`. `Discard` discards
   both outbound queues. *Rejected:* closing lane 1 separately (no close
   code or event for half a connection exists).
10. **`/3` hellos and welcomes carry wire version 4.** The hello/welcome
    layout is `/1`'s (`handshake.rs:237–317`); on `/3` its version byte must
    be `MOVEMENT_WIRE_VERSION` (4, `crates/crpg-net/src/protocol_v4.rs:27`),
    on `/1` and `/2` it stays 1 or 2 (`handshake.rs:328–334`). The public
    `Hello::encode/decode` and `Welcome::encode/decode` keep the `/1`/`/2`
    rule; the endpoints use crate-private framing-aware twins. *Rejected:*
    widening the public rule to 1–4 (a `/1` hello naming 4 would then reach
    the adapter as an event instead of closing at the transport, changing
    `/1` behaviour).
11. **(T150c) A `/3` binding needs a v4 invitation.** `bind_v4_peer` needs
    fact grants and pause rights (`tasks/T037.md:437–441`) that `Invitation`
    lacks (`crates/crpg-server/src/quic.rs:76–84`); adding fields to that
    all-public struct would break every struct literal of it in the
    existing suites. So `register_v4_invitation(V4Invitation)` stores them,
    and a `/3` hello presenting an invitation registered without them is
    refused `AuthRefused`. A v4 invitation on a `/1` or `/2` connection binds
    as today (the facts are unused). (Question 2.)
12. **(T150c) Receipts are taken only when the transport's lane-1 queue is
    empty.** Then every receipt taken fits (Reading 8), the adapter holds no
    receipt, and a client that stops reading lane 1 pushes back through the
    host: untaken receipts count as unreceipted (`tasks/T037.md:214–217`),
    so admission answers `QueueFull`, the adapter holds that frame
    (Reading 14), and QUIC flow control stalls the client. *Rejected:* an
    adapter receipt buffer with its own stall timer (a second slow-consumer
    rule and a second unbounded-by-construction buffer).
13. **(T150c) Lane-1 calls are traced in a side list that records their
    position.** The existing replay matches `HostCall` with no wildcard arm
    (`crates/crpg-server/tests/host_quic.rs:713–799`), so a new `HostCall`
    variant would be an existing-test edit. `QuicPumpReport.lane1` holds
    `Lane1Trace { at, call }`, `at` being `calls.len()` when the call was
    made, so a replay interleaves both lists exactly. *Rejected:* new
    `HostCall` variants; a side list without positions (lane-0 `ingest` and
    lane-1 `ingest_move` spend the same session buckets, so their order
    matters, `tasks/T037.md:710–717`).
14. **(T150c) Lane-1 dispositions mirror lane 0's.** A `Refused { code:
    QueueFull }` frame is held and retried before any later lane-1 frame of
    that connection (T023b's R5 hold, `crates/crpg-server/src/quic.rs:852–889`);
    `Unauthenticated` fences; every other refusal is dropped and counted.
    The two lanes drain independently: a held lane-0 frame does not stop
    lane-1 draining, nor the reverse (HM-10).
15. **(T150c) Samples are taken every pump and sent once.** Never held, never
    retried (L4; `tasks/T037.md:284–294`). A departed peer's receipts and
    samples are taken and discarded, as its lane-0 delivery is
    (`quic.rs:973–989`).
16. **(T150c) `QuicHost::advance` is a pass-through.** Lane-1 intents are
    decided only in `Host::advance` (`tasks/T037.md:765–768`), which
    `QuicHost` does not expose (it passes `tick` through, `quic.rs:1335–1340`).
    It is untraced, like `tick`.
17. **(T150a) Patch `0002` is the git blob of the T030 patch, byte for
    byte**, as ADR-0027 planned (`docs/adr/0027-vendored-crates-io-patches.md:72–74`).
    This worktree checks the file out with CRLF (`.gitattributes:5` sets only
    `-whitespace` for it), so it is copied with `git cat-file blob`, never from
    the working tree: blob SHA-256
    `32e186ea350cbb8a0e4a9cafd9b71e6b0aa2f1d065bb63d6ec2e45415dc9cc63`,
    11,017 bytes. It touches only `src/connection/spaces.rs`, which patch
    0001 does not, and adds no `#[test]` (its four `#[test]` lines are
    context). The Windows matrix compares the registry quinn-proto with
    **the vendored tree** (0001 + 0002), where T030's "patched" build had
    the dedup patch alone; the summary says so. (Question 3 is the H-dep
    approval ADR-0027 item 2 asks for.)

### T150b — `handshake.rs`
```rust
/// ALPN identifier: connection mapping version 3 — the `crpg-lane0/2` lane
/// stream, a second client-initiated bidi stream for lane 1, and DATAGRAM
/// samples downstream (T150). Served only by `QuicServer::bind_movement`.
pub const ALPN_LANE0_V3: &[u8] = b"crpg-lane0/3";
/// The lane-0 wire version every `crpg-lane0/3` hello and welcome carries
/// (protocol v4).
pub const MOVEMENT_WIRE_VERSION: u8 = 4;
/// Largest datagram, and largest downstream lane-1 stream frame, in bytes.
pub const MAX_DATAGRAM_BYTES: usize = 1_024;
/// Frames a server may queue on one connection's lane-1 outbound queue.
pub const LANE1_OUTBOUND_FRAMES: usize = 128;

const _: () = assert!(MOVEMENT_WIRE_VERSION == crpg_net::protocol_v4::PROTOCOL_VERSION);
const _: () = assert!(MAX_DATAGRAM_BYTES == crpg_net::movement_v4::MAX_MOVE_DOWN_BYTES);
const _: () = assert!(
    crpg_net::movement_v4::MAX_SAMPLE_HEADER_BYTES
        + crpg_net::movement_v4::MAX_SAMPLE_ENTRIES * crpg_net::movement_v4::MAX_SAMPLE_ENTRY_BYTES
        <= MAX_DATAGRAM_BYTES
);
const _: () = assert!(LANE1_OUTBOUND_FRAMES == crpg_net::protocol::POLICY_EGRESS_FRAMES_PER_PEER);
```
Crate-private (names for reviewers): `fn wire_version_ok(movement: bool,
version: u8) -> bool` (`movement`: `version == 4`; else 1 or 2);
`Hello::encode_for(&self, movement: bool)`, `Hello::decode_for(bytes,
movement: bool)`, `Welcome::encode_for(&self, movement: bool)`,
`Welcome::decode_for(bytes, movement: bool)`, each `pub(crate)` with the
existing precedence and the version test replaced by `wire_version_ok`; the
existing public `encode`/`decode` call them with `false` and behave exactly as
today. The doc of `Framing::V2` gains "a `crpg-lane0/3` connection also
reports `V2`: its lane stream is framed exactly as `/2`".

### T150b — `close.rs`
```rust
/// One connection's lane-1 and datagram accounting (T150). On a connection
/// that is not `crpg-lane0/3` every count is 0 and `max_datagram_bytes` is
/// `None`.
```

## Questions for the user
1. **How should a movement client and server find each other on the wire?**
   When a client connects, it and the server agree on a short label (the
   "ALPN") that names which wire rules they speak. Movement needs new rules
   (a second ordered channel for move orders and their answers, plus small
   "fire and forget" packets for positions), so it needs a new label,
   `crpg-lane0/3`. QUIC fixes some connection settings per server port
   before it knows the label. (a) **A separate server type that speaks only
   the new label** (`bind_movement`): old and new clients use different
   server types; a host that wants both opens two ports. Nothing an old
   client sees changes. (b) **Add the new label to today's two-label
   server** (`bind_dual`): one port for everyone, but old clients would then
   be offered the movement settings too (permission for a second channel
   they never open, and a "zero-size" datagram setting); ADR-0025's pinned
   settings for old clients change, and two existing rules need rewording.
   (c) **(a) with a different name**, `crpg-lane1/1`. *Recommend (a):* the
   only movement client planned is the Godot client (T151), which speaks v4
   anyway, and (a) changes nothing that existing tests and ADR-0025 pin.
2. **What does a player who joins through the movement server get to see,
   and may they pause?** A v4 session needs two operator settings that
   today's invitation does not carry: "fact grants" (whether the player may
   see each creature's health pools, effects, stats and tags) and "pause
   rights" (whether the player may pause or unpause). (a) **A v4 invitation
   that carries both** (`register_v4_invitation`); a movement client
   presenting an old-style invitation is refused. (b) **No new invitation
   type:** every movement player sees no facts and may not pause until a
   later task adds a way to set them. (c) **Add the two fields to today's
   invitation:** simplest to use, but every existing test that builds an
   invitation must be edited, which needs your permission. *Recommend (a):*
   it is additive, keeps every test untouched, and gives the Godot demo
   real health bars.
3. **May the vendored QUIC library carry the prepared "dedup window" patch,
   and must the Windows measurement pass before the movement transport
   merges?** QUIC throws away packets it thinks are duplicates. Its filter
   remembers only the last 129 packets, so under heavy reordering it can
   discard late but genuinely new packets, including position samples (an
   upstream bug, quinn #2710, closed unfixed). The prepared patch makes it
   remember 2,049 packets, costs 696 bytes per connection and changes no
   behaviour for in-order traffic. D23 scheduled it for "the first datagram
   lane", which is this task, and ADR-0027 says each vendored patch needs
   your own approval. (a) **Approve patch 0002, and merge T150b only after
   T150a's native Windows matrix passes** (zero duplicate discards in all
   39 runs). (b) **Approve the patch, but do not gate T150b on the
   matrix:** faster, but D23's "re-run on native Windows at that point" is
   then met after the fact. (c) **Decline:** T150b still works on clean
   networks, but D23 is unmet and must be revised, and samples may vanish
   under deep reordering. *Recommend (a):* it is what D23 decided, and the
   matrix is about 45 minutes on this machine.
