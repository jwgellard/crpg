# T024 — bounded versioned snapshot transfer (`crpg-net`)

## Task / Crate(s) / Purpose

Sole crate: `crpg-net`, Phase 4 after T021/T023b. **D11 selected** (see
[decision record](POST-T018-DECISIONS.md#d11--snapshot-transfer-separate-versioned-control-framing));
exact transfer layout remains. Promote
T018b's provisional test-local snapshot tracker into a production transfer
wire and bounded reassembler. This is a new capability, not a completed T018 feature.

## Interface — exact transfer/version gate

Append approved transfer protocol version, direction/lane and tag assignments,
snapshot identity/epoch, base tick and delivery cursor, total byte/entity/chunk
counts, chunk index/length/payload representation, completion/cancel/resync
messages, reassembler methods and exact errors/precedence before implementation.
Version may be a separate reviewed transfer subprotocol or a subsequent main
protocol version; do not bolt messages into frozen v1 or already-frozen v2.

Inherited bounds: one in flight per peer, at most 32 chunks, 1 MiB snapshot,
1024 visible entities, 5 s injected monotonic deadline (4,999 ms live;
5,000 expired). Hard wire bounds are versioned; tighter operational deadlines
are policy. **D11 must resolve packing:** 32 chunks each with only a single
4 KiB byte field cannot carry 1 MiB. Options include bounded multiple fields
within the 64 KiB frame ceiling or a separately reviewed larger field cap;
do not silently claim an impossible max-size positive test.

## Behaviour

Only allowlisted permitted replica facts enter the payload; a World/save blob
is not a snapshot. Validate all lengths/counts/checked arithmetic before
allocation. Bound both per-peer and aggregate staging; approve the host-total
envelope in D11 rather than treating eight peers as unlimited storage.

Define out-of-order chunks, identical duplicates, conflicting duplicates,
mixed epochs/ids, cancellation and missing chunks. Recommended: identical
duplicates are idempotent, conflicts discard the transfer, neither extends
deadline; expiry frees all reservations. Full validated assembly publishes
atomically; incomplete/failed transfers leave prior replica intact. Pin the
base-cursor/delta barrier so concurrent deltas cannot be lost, double-applied
or applied to the wrong snapshot. Supersession/revocation must not republish
now-hidden facts. Byte assembly alone is not resync correctness.

## Constraints

[Standing rules](POST-T018-RULES.md) apply. Net only, no authoritative World
mutation, host authentication or persistence file format. No compression/bulk
package transfer in this slice. Each new hard cap/version is explicitly reviewed;
existing v1 fixtures stay untouched. No new dependencies without approval.

## Test

Public `snapshot_transfer` suite with genuine max-valid/first-invalid byte,
chunk/entity/frame cases, hostile lengths, second-in-flight refusal, aggregate
cap, duplicates/conflicts/reorder/missing chunks, 4999/5000 ms boundary and
cleanup/retry positive control. Assert atomic publication and base-cursor delta
cutover, old/mixed epoch refusal, hidden-field exclusion. Independent replica
oracle detects dropped/corrupt required chunks; verify v1 rejects transfer tags.

#### S§1.2 The D11 packing problem, in numbers

- One 4,096-byte field per chunk: 32 × 4,096 = 131,072 bytes, one eighth of
  1,048,576. The outline's impossibility is real.
- D11's eight 4,096-byte segments per chunk: 8 × 4,096 = 32,768 bytes, and
  32 × 32,768 = 1,048,576 **exactly**, with no slack. The maximal snapshot
  needs every chunk full.
- No existing cap is raised: segments stay at `MAX_WIRE_BYTES_FIELD`, frames
  stay under `MAX_DELTA_FRAME_BYTES`.

#### S§1.4 The largest valid body is one chunk

The allowlisted facts (S§5.4) are exactly today's state vocabulary:
presence, health/max/dead and turn. At 1,024 entities with every fact and
maximal values the body is 22,552 bytes, so **no valid T024 body exceeds
6 % of the 1 MiB cap, and none needs more than one 32 KiB chunk** (6 chunks
at 4 KiB). The 32-chunk / 1 MiB envelope is a versioned transport ceiling
for later fact vocabularies (T027b private-field grants, D13 movement). It
cannot be reached with a valid body today. S§9 and S§16 Q2 say how the
full-size claim is proved honestly.

#### S§1.5 Throughput against the 5 s deadline

- An `InMemoryTransport` built with `RateCaps::v1()` (the intent-ingress
  rates: 64 KiB/s, 128 KiB burst) admitted the 33 frames of a full 1 MiB
  transfer only at **14,050 ms** (measured, 50 ms retry steps; analytic
  (1,050,136 − 131,072) / 65,536 s = 14.02 s). Downstream has no v1 rate
  policy; the fabric applies one rate set to every `send_to`. The suite's
  fabric cases therefore use the existing `generous_rates()` pattern.
- A full transfer takes 33 of the 128 per-peer egress frames and 50.1 % of
  the 2 MiB per-peer egress bytes, and 8 such transfers fit the 16 MiB host
  egress. It exceeds the client's 1 MiB QUIC stream window by 1,692 bytes,
  so the client must read while it arrives, which is ordinary flow control.

#### S§1.6 What exists today

- No transfer wire. `tests/transport.rs` `snapshot_reassembly_bounds` is
  T018b's test-local tracker (1 in flight, 32 chunks, 4,999/5,000 ms). It
  stays as it is (out of scope: T018b's historical contract).
- `RejectionCode::SnapshotExpired` (20) is a v1 receipt code. Receipts are
  per command, and T024 does not use it.
- The T022 host resynchronises by narrowing: `set_grants` bumps a private
  delivery generation, **restarts `event_seq` at 1** and stages
  `full_state_ops` as ordinary delta frames, and T023b fences the connection
  because bytes already handed to QUIC cannot be recalled (ADR-0025, R4).
  Delta frames carry no generation (v1/v2 are frozen), so a host that reset
  `event_seq` without fencing would make old in-flight deltas look new. S§11
  item 2 makes "never reset within an epoch" a requirement on the host
  wiring.
- Measured baseline at this tip: `cargo test -p crpg-net --locked` 81 passed
  (`codec` 28, `conformance` 22, `events_v2` 6, `transport` 25).

### S§2. Decisions this contract makes

1. **A separate transfer subprotocol, not a main protocol version.** First
   byte `TRANSFER_VERSION = 0x81` ("transfer subprotocol version 1": the high
   bit marks the subprotocol, so main versions stay below 0x80). Lane byte
   `LANE_SNAPSHOT = 2` (lane 1 stays reserved for D13 movement). The
   v1/v2 modules, bytes and fixtures are untouched (S§16 Q1).
2. **Tags disjoint from every combat tag**: downstream 0x10 `Begin`, 0x11
   `Chunk`, 0x12 `Cancel`; upstream 0x20 `Published`, 0x21 `Resync`. A tag of
   the other direction is `WrongDirection`; any other is `UnknownMessage`.
3. **Packing**: a chunk is 1..=8 segments of exactly 4,096 bytes, except
   that the final segment of a chunk may be shorter. The sender picks
   `segments_per_chunk` (1..=8) in `Begin`. That fixes every chunk's length,
   so segmentation is canonical and a chunk has one valid encoding
   (S§16 Q2).
4. **Two layers.** A byte layer, `TransferAssembly`, proves the full
   1 MiB / 32-chunk envelope with exact bytes. A receiver layer,
   `ReassemblyPool`, adds the deadline, the budgets, body validation,
   atomic publication and the delta cutover gate.
5. **Cutover by delivery cursor.** A snapshot names the last delta
   `event_seq` it includes. Deltas at or below it are superseded, and deltas
   above it are held until publication. Publication and the deltas it
   releases leave through one FIFO update queue, so nothing can be applied
   to the wrong base (S§10.7, S§16 Q3).
6. **Fail closed.** After any failure, and after a cancel, the receiver
   holds deltas until a new snapshot publishes (S§16 Q4).
7. **No integrity digest.** QUIC/TLS already protects the bytes, and a
   digest would need a hash dependency `crpg-net` does not have. Dropped or
   corrupted chunks are caught by the independent test oracle (case 30), and
   inconsistent duplicates are caught by byte comparison.
8. **Net only.** The real transport channel and the host wiring are
   follow-on tasks (S§15, S§16 Q5).

### S§4. Version, lane, direction and tags

| Item | Value | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| First byte | `0x81` | `decode_down`/`decode_up` refuse any other byte with `UnsupportedVersion`; the v1 and v2 codecs already refuse 0x81 the same way |
| Lane | `2` | anything else is `WrongDirection` |
| Downstream (host → client), at most 65,536 B | `0x10` Begin, `0x11` Chunk, `0x12` Cancel | `decode_down` reads `0x20`/`0x21` as `WrongDirection` |
| Upstream (client → host), at most 4,096 B | `0x20` Published, `0x21` Resync | `decode_up` reads `0x10..=0x12` as `WrongDirection` |
| Any other tag byte | — | `UnknownMessage` |

Demultiplexing happens at transport/channel selection (D11). A caller picks
the codec for the channel a frame arrived on. No decoder inspects a frame to
guess its protocol, and none falls back to another (ADR-0019).

### S§5. Wire layout (exact field order)

Encoding uses the existing `postcard` primitives: a raw byte for `u8` and
`bool`, LEB128 varints for `u16`/`u32`/`u64`, and `[u8; 16]` raw. Each frame
starts with `0x81`, then `2`, then the tag.

#### S§5.1 Downstream

| Tag | Fields after the tag, in order |
|---|---|
| `0x10` Begin | `epoch [u8;16]`, `transfer u64` (≠ 0), `base_tick u64`, `cursor u64` (≤ `SEQ_LAST`), `total_bytes` varint (1..=1,048,576), `entity_count` varint (≤ 1,024), `segments_per_chunk u8` (1..=8), `chunk_count u8` (1..=32, = ⌈`total_bytes` / (`segments_per_chunk` × 4,096)⌉) |
| `0x11` Chunk | `epoch [u8;16]`, `transfer u64` (≠ 0), `index u8` (< 32), `segment_count u8` (1..=8), then `segment_count` × (`len` varint (1..=4,096), `len` bytes); every segment but the last is exactly 4,096 bytes |
| `0x12` Cancel | `epoch [u8;16]`, `transfer u64` (≠ 0), `reason u8` (1 `Superseded`, 2 `Aborted`) |

#### S§5.2 Upstream

| Tag | Fields after the tag, in order |
|---|---|
| `0x20` Published | `epoch [u8;16]`, `transfer u64` (≠ 0) |
| `0x21` Resync | `epoch [u8;16]`, `after u64` (0 = no transfer seen), `reason u8` (1 `Expired`, 2 `Invalid`, 3 `Overflow`) |

#### S§5.3 Canonical chunking

`chunk_size = segments_per_chunk × 4,096`. Chunk `i` carries payload bytes
`[i × chunk_size, min((i + 1) × chunk_size, total_bytes))`, so every chunk
but the last is exactly `chunk_size` bytes. Its segments are those bytes cut
at 4,096. A chunk therefore has exactly one valid encoding, and an
identical duplicate is byte-identical.

#### S§5.4 Snapshot body (the reassembled payload)

| Field | Encoding |
|---|---|
| `entity_count` | varint, ≤ 1,024 |
| per entity, strictly ascending `NetId` | `entity u64` (≠ 0), `health_present bool`, then if present `health u32`, `max_health u32`, `dead bool` |
| `turn_present` | `bool`, then if present `active_present bool`, `active u64` if present (≠ 0, and one of the body's entities), `round u64` |

These are the only facts a v1 transfer body can carry. They are exactly
the v1 state ops (`EntityEnter`, `Health`, `Turn`), the facts T022's
`full_state_ops` sends today. Events, receipts, spawn/despawn/death notices,
raw `EntityId`s, rolls, grants and any World or save bytes have no field.
An absent fact is a `0` flag with no payload bytes, never a zero-filled
value. New facts need a new transfer version (`0x82`) and their own review.
`MAX_ENCODED_BODY_BYTES = 22,552` = 2 + 1,024 × 22 + 22.

#### S§5.5 Literal examples (all-zero epoch)

- Minimal `Begin` (id 1, base 0, cursor 0, total 2, 0 entities, 1 segment,
  1 chunk): `81 02 10` + 16 × `00` + `01 00 00 02 00 01 01` (26 bytes).
- Its single `Chunk` (empty body `00 00`): `81 02 11` + 16 × `00` +
  `01 00 01 02 00 00` (25 bytes).
- `Cancel` (id 1, `Superseded`): `81 02 12` + 16 × `00` + `01 01` (21 bytes).
- `Published` (id 1): `81 02 20` + 16 × `00` + `01` (20 bytes).
- `Resync` (none seen, `Expired`): `81 02 21` + 16 × `00` + `00 01` (21 bytes).
- Body with entity 1 at health 10/10 alive and no turn:
  `01 01 01 0A 0A 00 00` (7 bytes); with turn (active 1, round 3) the last
  byte becomes `01 01 01 03`.

### S§6. Codec rules and error precedence

Errors are the existing six `CodecError` variants, re-exported.

1. **Whole-frame cap first, before any read**: `decode_down` input over
   65,536 bytes and `decode_up` input over 4,096 bytes are `FrameTooLarge`.
2. **First byte**: not `0x81` is `UnsupportedVersion`, and empty input is
   `Malformed`.
3. **Lane**: not `2` is `WrongDirection`.
4. **Tag**: the other direction's tag is `WrongDirection`, any other unknown
   tag `UnknownMessage`.
5. **Fields left to right, each checked when read** (the T021 rule). A
   failure stops the decode.
   - A value above its cap is `LimitExceeded`: `total_bytes` >
     1,048,576, `entity_count` > 1,024, `segments_per_chunk` > 8,
     `chunk_count` > 32, `index` ≥ 32, `segment_count` > 8, segment `len` >
     4,096. Count and length fields are read as `u64` varints and compared
     before any allocation, so a hostile 10-byte value is `LimitExceeded`,
     never a wrap or a reservation. A varint that overflows `u64` is
     `Malformed` (codec_v2's tenth-byte rule).
   - Zero or inconsistent is `Malformed`: `transfer` 0, `cursor` >
     `SEQ_LAST`, `total_bytes` 0, `segments_per_chunk` 0, `chunk_count` 0 or
     not ⌈total / chunk_size⌉, `segment_count` 0, a segment `len` of 0, a
     non-final segment shorter than 4,096, a body shorter than its `len`.
   - An unassigned `reason` byte is `UnknownMessage` (the `RejectionCode`
     precedent).
6. **Trailing bytes** are `Malformed`.

Segment length is checked against 4,096 **before** body availability. A
chunk payload grows by `extend` from already-bounded segments, so at most
32,768 bytes are ever copied from one frame. Nothing calls `with_capacity`
on an untrusted count.

**Encode** checks the same rules in the same field order, then the frame
cap, and never returns partial bytes. For a `Chunk`, `index` ≥ 32 is
`LimitExceeded`, empty `bytes` is `Malformed` and more than 32,768 bytes is
`LimitExceeded`, in that order. `BeginTransfer::validate` is the `Begin`
routine, shared by `encode_down`, `TransferAssembly::new` and the receiver.

**Body** (`decode_body`): input over 1,048,576 bytes is `LimitExceeded`;
`entity_count` > 1,024 is `LimitExceeded` before the vector grows (it grows
by push); a `NetId` of 0, a non-ascending or repeated `NetId`, a flag byte
other than 0/1, an `active` not among the entities, a `u32` varint overflow,
truncation and trailing bytes are `Malformed`. `encode_body` checks the count
(`LimitExceeded`), then the ascending order, then `active` membership
(`Malformed`). A valid body re-encodes byte-identically.

### S§7. Hard caps and policy limits

Wire hard maxima are versioned: raising one is a new transfer version and a
review. Policy limits are tightening-only (`validate` refuses looser
values).

| Bound | Value | Kind | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Snapshot bytes | 1,048,576 | wire | `MAX_SNAPSHOT_BYTES` (T018a), D11 |
| Chunks | 32 | wire | `MAX_SNAPSHOT_CHUNKS`, D11 |
| Visible entities per body | 1,024 | wire | `MAX_VISIBLE_ENTITIES`, D11 |
| Segment bytes | 4,096 | wire | `MAX_WIRE_BYTES_FIELD`, D11 (not raised) |
| Segments per chunk | 8 | wire | D11 |
| Chunk payload | 32,768 | wire | 8 × 4,096; × 32 = 1,048,576 (compile-time check) |
| Downstream frame | 65,536 (max used 32,815) | wire | `MAX_DELTA_FRAME_BYTES` |
| Upstream frame | 4,096 (max used 30) | wire | `MAX_INTENT_FRAME_BYTES` |
| Largest valid v1 body | 22,552 | derived | S§5.4 |
| In flight per receiver | 1 | policy (fixed) | `POLICY_SNAPSHOT_IN_FLIGHT_PER_PEER`, D11 |
| Deadline from accepted `Begin` | 5,000 ms (1..=5,000) | policy | `POLICY_SNAPSHOT_REASSEMBLY_SECS`, D11 |
| Receivers per pool | 8 (1..=8) | policy | `POLICY_PROOF_PEERS`, D10's eight-peer envelope |
| Staging per receiver | 1,048,576 (22,552..=) | policy | D11 "1 MiB/peer" |
| Staging, whole pool | 8,388,608 (≥ per receiver) | policy | D11 "8 MiB aggregate" |

| Held + queued deltas, whole pool | 16,777,216 B (≥ per receiver) | policy | `POLICY_EGRESS_BYTES_HOST` |
| Decoded bodies per receiver | ≤ 2 at once, each ≤ 1,024 entities | structural | S§10.6; about 24 KiB each (24-byte entity on the measured layout) |

Worst-case pool memory at v1: 8 MiB staging + 16 MiB held deltas + 8 × 2
decoded bodies (about 0.4 MiB) + fixed per-receiver metadata (a 32-bit chunk

(all `usize` except `MAX_SEGMENTS_PER_CHUNK: u8`):
`MAX_SEGMENT_BYTES = MAX_WIRE_BYTES_FIELD`, `MAX_SEGMENTS_PER_CHUNK = 8`,
`MAX_CHUNK_BYTES = 32_768`, `MAX_DOWN_FRAME_BYTES = MAX_DELTA_FRAME_BYTES`,
`MAX_UP_FRAME_BYTES = MAX_INTENT_FRAME_BYTES`, `MAX_ENCODED_BODY_BYTES =
22_552`, `POLICY_SNAPSHOT_STAGING_BYTES_PER_PEER = MAX_SNAPSHOT_BYTES`,
`POLICY_SNAPSHOT_STAGING_BYTES_HOST = 8 * 1_048_576`, with

### S§8. Interface (exact public Rust)

Every new struct and enum derives `Debug, Clone, PartialEq, Eq` (no serde).
Error types implement `Display` as `<VariantName> at transfer/<area>`, with no

that a refusal leaves the whole pool unchanged with `assert_eq!`.
`TransferAssembly` derives the same.

`validate` checks fields in declaration order. A field below its floor
(zero, under 22,552 / 65,536, or a total under its per-receiver value) is
`InvalidLimits`; above `v1()` it is `LoosensPolicy`. Display:
`<Variant> at transfer/limits: <field>`.

### S§9. Byte layer (`TransferAssembly`)

- `new` runs `validate` and then allocates exactly `total_bytes` (at most
  1,048,576) once. Nothing else allocates in proportion to wire values.
- `accept` checks in this order: `(epoch, transfer)` differs from the
  `Begin` (`WrongTransfer`); `index >= chunk_count` (`OutOfRange`);
  `bytes.len() != chunk_len(index)` (`WrongLength`); chunk already received
  with different bytes (`Conflict`) or identical bytes (`Ok(Duplicate)`).
  Otherwise it copies the bytes to the chunk's offset and returns `Stored`,
  or `Complete` for the last missing chunk. An error leaves the assembly
  unchanged.
- `into_bytes` hands the buffer over by ownership, without a copy. This is
  the layer that proves the full 1 MiB / 32-chunk envelope (case 10).

### S§10. Receiver behaviour (`ReassemblyPool`)

#### S§10.1 Identity

- A receiver is bound to one session `epoch` and one delta wire version for
  its life. A new epoch is a new receiver (T025a owns resume).
- `retired_through` is a high-water mark. Every transfer id at or below it
  can never begin, store a chunk or publish again. A transfer id is retired
  when it publishes, fails or is cancelled, and by a `Cancel` naming a
  higher id.
- `add_receiver` refuses `ReceiversFull` at `max_receivers` (or once the
  `u32` id space is used up). A new receiver starts with `next_event_seq`
  1, nothing retired, and the mode the caller chooses: `Live` for a session
  that boots from deltas (T022 today), `AwaitSnapshot` for one that must
  install a snapshot first.

#### S§10.2 `offer_transfer` precedence (first failure wins)

1. Clamp `now_ms` to at least the pool's last value, then run the
   pool-wide expiry sweep (S§10.5).
2. Unknown receiver: `UnknownReceiver`.
3. `decode_down`: `Codec(e)`.
4. `epoch` ≠ the receiver's epoch: `WrongEpoch`.
5. A frame naming the transfer this call's sweep just expired:
   `Failed(Expired)`. The failure itself (S§10.4) already happened in the
   sweep; this only reports it to the caller whose frame arrived too late.
6. By message, S§10.3.

#### S§10.3 Messages

**`Begin`**, in this order:

1. Same id as the in-flight transfer: identical, `Ok(Duplicate)` (deadline
   unchanged); any field different, `Failed(Conflict)`.
2. Id ≤ `retired_through`: `StaleTransfer`.
3. Another transfer in flight: `InFlight` (one in flight per receiver, D11).
   Supersession is an explicit `Cancel` first (S§11 item 4).
4. `cursor + 1 < next_event_seq`, or `base_tick` below the published
   `base_tick`: `BehindReplica`. The snapshot would roll back deltas already
   released.
5. `total_bytes` > `staging_bytes_per_receiver`: `StagingFull`.
6. Pool staging + `total_bytes` > `staging_bytes_total`: `AggregateFull`.
7. Accept. Reserve `total_bytes`, create the `TransferAssembly`, set
   `deadline_ms = now.saturating_add(limits.deadline_ms)`, clear `resync`,
   and return `Ok(Begun)`.

**`Chunk`**:

1. For the in-flight id, `TransferAssembly::accept`. Then:
   - `WrongLength`, `OutOfRange` or `Conflict` becomes `Failed(Conflict)`;
   - `Duplicate` becomes `Ok(Duplicate)`, with no deadline extension;
   - `Stored` becomes `Ok(Stored { received, chunk_count })`;
   - `Complete` runs publication (S§10.6).
2. Id ≤ `retired_through`: `StaleTransfer`.
3. Otherwise (a transfer that never began, or whose `Begin` was refused):
   `UnknownTransfer`.

**`Cancel { transfer: t }`**:

- If a transfer with id ≤ `t` is in flight, discard it (free the
  reservation), set `retired_through = max(retired_through, t)`, set the
  mode to `AwaitSnapshot` and return `Ok(Cancelled { discarded: true })`.
  `resync` is not set: the host started the cancel.
- Otherwise set `retired_through = max(retired_through, t)` and return
  `Ok(Cancelled { discarded: false })`. A repeat is an idempotent no-op.
  Cancelling an id that already published does not touch the queued
  snapshot: published facts are not retractable (ADR-0022).

#### S§10.4 Failure

`Failed(reason)` does this atomically:

1. Discard the in-flight transfer (if any) and free its reservation.
2. Retire its id.
3. Set the mode to `AwaitSnapshot` and `resync = Some(reason.resync_reason())`.
4. For `Overflow`, also drop every **held** (not yet queued) delta.
   Already-queued updates stay: they were valid when released.

The prior replica stays intact: nothing of the failed transfer reaches the
queue. Every later frame for that id is `StaleTransfer`.

#### S§10.5 Deadline (4,999 / 5,000)

- The clock is the caller's injected monotonic milliseconds, never
  `SystemTime` and never a tick. A smaller `now_ms` than the pool has seen
  is clamped up, so time never runs backwards and expiry never un-happens.
- A transfer accepted at `t` is live while `now < t + deadline_ms` and
  expired once `now ≥ t + deadline_ms`. With v1 that is live through
  `t + 4,999` and expired from `t + 5,000`.
- Duplicates, retries and refused frames never move the deadline (D11
  "no retry extension").
- Every `offer_transfer`, `offer_delta` and `poll` call first sweeps every
  receiver in ascending `ReceiverId` order and fails each overdue transfer
  with `Expired` (S§10.4). A stale reservation therefore never blocks
  another receiver's `Begin`. `poll` returns the receivers it expired.
- This sweep is the only state change a refused call can make. Tests compare
  whole pools at an unchanged `now_ms`.

#### S§10.6 Atomic publication

On the chunk that completes the assembly, in this order and in this one
call:

1. `into_bytes` (ownership, no copy), then `decode_body`. A body error
   becomes `Failed(InvalidBody(e))`. `body.entities.len() !=
   entity_count` becomes `Failed(InvalidBody(Malformed))`.
2. Free the reservation, retire the id and clear the in-flight transfer.
3. If the update queue already holds a `Snapshot`, clear the whole queue.
   Its deltas are all at or below this cursor (S§10.3 step 4), so the new
   snapshot supersedes them. This keeps at most one queued snapshot, and so
   at most two decoded bodies per receiver.
4. Drop every held delta with `event_seq ≤ cursor`.
5. Set `published`, `next_event_seq = cursor + 1`, mode `Live`, `resync = None`.
6. Push `ReplicaUpdate::Snapshot(publication)`.
7. Move held deltas from `next_event_seq` upward to the queue while they are
   contiguous.
8. Return `Ok(Published { transfer, released })`.

If step 1 fails, nothing from steps 2–8 happens beyond S§10.4.

#### S§10.7 The cutover barrier

A delta is **releasable** when the mode is `Live` and either no transfer is
in flight or its `event_seq ≤` the in-flight cursor. The queue is FIFO and
is the only way updates leave the pool. So:

- deltas at or below the cursor that were queued before publication are
  popped before the snapshot (they apply to the old base, which the
  snapshot then replaces);
- deltas above the cursor are never queued before the snapshot;
- a delta at or below a published cursor that arrives later is
  `Superseded`.

No delta is applied twice, none is applied to the wrong base, and none is
skipped. A gap is never released past, so the stream never reports success
with a delta missing (D11).

Correctness needs one property of the transport and host: **a peer's
transfer frames and delta frames share one total order** (S§11 item 3). With
that, `BehindReplica` cannot occur. Without it, the receiver refuses rather
than corrupts (S§16 Q3).

#### S§10.8 `offer_delta` precedence

1. Clamp and sweep (S§10.5).
2. Unknown receiver: `UnknownReceiver`.
3. Decode with the receiver's `DeltaWire` (`codec::decode_delta` or
   `codec_v2::decode_delta`): `Codec(e)`. A v1 frame to a V2 receiver is
   `Codec(UnsupportedVersion)`, and the reverse too.
4. `event_seq` outside `1..=SEQ_LAST`: `DeltaSequence`.
5. `event_seq < next_event_seq`: `Ok(Superseded)`.
6. Already held: identical bytes, `Ok(Duplicate)`; different bytes,
   `Failed(Conflict)`.
7. Storing it would pass `delta_frames_per_receiver`,
   `delta_bytes_per_receiver` or the pool's `delta_bytes_total` (held plus
   queued, encoded length): `Failed(Overflow)`. The delta is dropped and
   nothing is skipped: the gap it leaves is healed only by a later snapshot.
8. Releasable and equal to `next_event_seq`: queue it, then drain contiguous
   releasable held deltas, and return `Ok(Released { count })`.
9. Otherwise hold it: `Ok(Buffered)`.

#### S§10.9 Supersession, revocation and hidden-field exclusion

- **Exclusion by construction.** A body can carry only the S§5.4 facts. An
  ungranted fact is `None` and contributes one `0` byte, whatever its hidden
  value. `active` must name an entity in the body, so a turn can never name
  a hidden entity. The codec checks shape, not entitlement: building the
  body from granted facts only is the host's job (S§11 item 6, T027b).
- **Revocation (D11, D14).** The host sends `Cancel(Aborted)` for an
  in-flight transfer that would newly disclose a now-forbidden fact. That
  id is then retired, so its remaining or duplicated chunks are
  `StaleTransfer` and it can never publish. The host then builds a new body
  under the new grants.
- **Supersession.** A newer snapshot needs a higher id after `Cancel` or
  after publication. A lower or retired id, or a base behind the replica,
  is refused (`StaleTransfer`, `BehindReplica`).

#### S§10.10 Refusals versus failures

| Refused (nothing changes) | Failed (S§10.4) |
|---|---|
| `Codec`, `UnknownReceiver`, `ReceiversFull`, `WrongEpoch`, `StaleTransfer`, `UnknownTransfer`, `InFlight`, `BehindReplica`, `StagingFull`, `AggregateFull`, `DeltaSequence` | `Failed(Conflict)`, `Failed(InvalidBody(_))`, `Failed(Expired)`, `Failed(Overflow)` |

The client reads `status().resync` and, if it is set, sends
`TransferUp::Resync { after: Some(retired id) or None, reason }`. After a
publication it sends `TransferUp::Published`. A refused `Begin` changes
nothing in the pool, so the caller answers it itself with a `Resync`
(`Overflow` for `StagingFull`/`AggregateFull`, `Invalid` otherwise) and the
host is never left waiting (S§11 item 4). Net never sends: the caller owns
its transport.

### S§11. Sender rules and host requirements

These are requirements on the later host wiring (S§16 Q5). T024 can check
only what its pool sees.

1. Transfer ids are strictly increasing per epoch, from 1, and never
   reused. They need not be contiguous.
2. **`event_seq` never resets within an epoch.** A snapshot's `cursor` is
   the last `event_seq` issued before capture, and later deltas are above
   it. T022's narrowing reset to 1 must change when narrowing becomes a
   seamless snapshot (S§1.6).
3. In the peer's single total order, a `Begin` precedes every delta above
   its cursor, and a transfer's chunks follow its `Begin`.
4. The host starts a new transfer only after `Published` or `Resync` for
   the previous one, or after sending `Cancel` for it.
5. On revocation that touches an in-flight transfer, the host sends
   `Cancel(Aborted)`, stops sending that id's chunks and captures a new
   body under the new grants (D11, D14).
6. A body holds only facts granted at capture: presence makes an entity,
   the health grant adds `health`, the `turn_state` grant adds `turn`, and
   `active` is `None` unless that entity is present.
7. Deltas after capture wait in egress under the existing caps. Overflow
   means `Cancel` and a fresh snapshot, or a fence; never a skipped delta.
8. `plan_transfer` produces canonical frames. A host that encodes frames
   itself must produce the same bytes.

### S§12. Tests (`crates/crpg-net/tests/snapshot_transfer.rs`, public API only)

Every refusal has a positive control and a whole-pool `assert_eq!` against a
clone taken before (S§10.5). The oracle is test-local and independent: it
applies popped updates to a `BTreeMap` replica and compares it per fact with
hand-authored expected facts. It never uses a production projection, and
there is no full World hash. The host stand-in builds bodies from a fixture
table of facts and grants. Pairs are written max-valid / first-invalid.

1. `wire_identifiers_and_caps_are_pinned`: every S§7/S§8.1 constant;
   `0x81` ∉ {1, 2}; transfer tags disjoint from intent tags {0, 1} and
   delta tags 0..=10; 32,768 × 32 = 1,048,576; `MAX_ENCODED_BODY_BYTES` =
   22,552; `TransferLimits::v1()` field by field; the v1 constants
   unchanged.
2. `literal_byte_oracles`: every S§5.5 example encodes to its literal bytes
   and decodes back; a 4,097-byte chunk is two segments (4,096 + 1).
3. `max_frames_fit_the_frame_ceilings`: `Begin` at maxima is 56 bytes; a
   full chunk at id `u64::MAX` is 32,815 ≤ 65,536; `Cancel` / `Published` /
   `Resync` maxima are 30 / 29 / 30; encode of a 32,768-byte chunk succeeds
   and of 32,769 is `LimitExceeded`; an empty chunk is `Malformed`.
4. `down_decode_precedence`: 65,537 bytes is `FrameTooLarge` even with a bad
   version; versions 1, 2, 0x80, 0x82 are `UnsupportedVersion`; lanes 0, 1, 3
   are `WrongDirection`; tags 0x20/0x21 are `WrongDirection`; 0x00, 0x08,
   0x13, 0xFF are `UnknownMessage`; `Cancel` reasons 0 and 3 are
   `UnknownMessage`; combinations report the earliest stage.
5. `up_decode_precedence`: 4,097 bytes is `FrameTooLarge`; tags
   0x10..=0x12 are `WrongDirection`; `Resync` reasons 0 and 4 are
   `UnknownMessage`; trailing bytes are `Malformed`.
6. `begin_bounds`: `transfer` 1 / 0 (`Malformed`); `cursor` `SEQ_LAST` /
   `u64::MAX`; `total_bytes` 1,048,576 / 1,048,577 (`LimitExceeded`), 0
   (`Malformed`); `entity_count` 1,024 / 1,025; `segments_per_chunk` 8 / 9,
   0; `chunk_count` 32 / 33, 0, and ⌈⌉ ± 1 (`Malformed`); hostile 10-byte
   varints are `LimitExceeded`; an overflowing varint is `Malformed`.
   `encode_down` and `TransferAssembly::new` give the same errors in the
   same order for every value their field types can hold.
7. `chunk_bounds_and_hostile_lengths`: `index` 31 / 32; `segment_count`
   8 / 9, 0; segment `len` 4,096 / 4,097 (`LimitExceeded` with no body
   bytes present); `len` 0; a non-final 4,095; a truncated body;
   tenth-byte overflow; a `len` of `u64::MAX` (`LimitExceeded`); one
   trailing byte.
8. `body_codec_bounds`: the maximal body (1,024 entities, every fact,
   maximal values) is 22,552 bytes, round-trips and re-encodes
   byte-identically; 1,025 entities is `LimitExceeded` on encode and on
   decode (count only, no entity bytes); repeated, descending and zero
   `NetId`s; flag byte 2; `active` absent from the entities; truncation;
   one trailing byte; input of 1,048,577 bytes.
9. `transfer_and_lane_zero_refuse_each_other`: the v1 and v2
   `decode_intent`/`decode_delta` refuse every transfer frame with
   `UnsupportedVersion`, and `decode_down`/`decode_up` refuse v1/v2 frames
   the same way; a valid v1 delta whose op tag is 0x10, 0x11, 0x12, 0x20
   or 0x21 is `UnknownMessage` in v1 and, separately, in v2; a valid v1
   intent with body tag 0x10 is `UnknownMessage`.
10. `full_size_byte_assembly`: a 1,048,576-byte payload (byte `i` =
    `(i × 31 + 7) mod 251`) at 8 segments per chunk is 32 chunks; every
    frame is ≤ 65,536; delivered in reverse it completes on the last chunk,
    and `into_bytes` equals the payload. The same at 1 segment per chunk
    with 131,072 bytes (32 × 4,096).
11. `assembly_duplicate_conflict_shape`: an identical duplicate is
    `Duplicate`; different bytes is `Conflict`; `index = chunk_count`
    (below 32) is `OutOfRange`; a non-final chunk 1 byte short and a final
    chunk 1 byte long are `WrongLength`; a wrong epoch or id is
    `WrongTransfer`; after each error the assembly equals its clone;
    `into_bytes` while incomplete returns `Err` with the same assembly.
12. `limits_validate_tightening_only`: `v1()` is valid; each field at v1 + 1
    is `LoosensPolicy { field }`; each floor − 1 (0, 22,551, 65,535, a total
    one below its per-receiver value) is `InvalidLimits { field }`; with two
    bad fields the first in declaration order is reported; `new` refuses
    invalid limits.
13. `max_valid_body_publishes_atomically`: the maximal body at 1 segment
    per chunk (6 chunks, shuffled) and at 8 (1 chunk). Before the last
    chunk the status shows 5 of 6 received, nothing queued and 22,552
    staged. The last chunk returns `Published`; `pop_update` yields the
    exact body; staging is back to 0; `retired_through` is the id; the
    `Published` frame round-trips through `encode_up`/`decode_up`.
14. `second_in_flight_refused`: `Begin(2)` while 1 is in flight is
    `InFlight` with the pool unchanged; 1 publishes; `Begin(2)` is then
    accepted; `Begin(1)` is `StaleTransfer`.
15. `staging_caps_per_receiver_and_aggregate`: at v1, eight receivers hold
    a 1,048,576-byte transfer each at once (8,388,608 staged), and a ninth
    `add_receiver` is `ReceiversFull`. With per-receiver 22,552, `Begin` of
    22,552 is accepted and 22,553 is `StagingFull`. With the pool total
    tightened to 1,048,576 + 22,552, a second receiver's 22,552 fits
    exactly and one byte more is `AggregateFull`. Publish, expiry, cancel
    and `remove_receiver` each give the room back.
16. `duplicates_idempotent_no_deadline_extension`: a duplicate `Begin` and a
    duplicate chunk at `t + 4,000` return `Duplicate`, `deadline_ms` is
    unchanged, and the last chunk at `t + 5,000` is `Failed(Expired)`.
17. `conflicts_discard_retire_and_flag`: a conflicting chunk, a conflicting
    `Begin` (same id, other cursor), an `index` past `chunk_count` and a
    wrong-length chunk are each `Failed(Conflict)`. Each frees staging,
    retires the id, sets `resync` to `Invalid` and the mode to
    `AwaitSnapshot`, and queues nothing; a later chunk of that id is
    `StaleTransfer`; the earlier published snapshot is still the oracle's
    state. A higher `Begin` clears `resync` and publishes.
18. `reorder_and_missing_chunks`: forward, reverse, interleaved and 64
    seeded permutations of 6 chunks all publish the same body. With chunk 3
    missing, `poll(t + 4,999)` returns nothing and `poll(t + 5,000)` returns
    the receiver; nothing is ever queued.
19. `deadline_boundary_4999_5000`: the last chunk at `t + 4,999`
    publishes. In a separate pool the last chunk at `t + 5,000` is
    `Failed(Expired)` with nothing queued and staging 0, and the next chunk
    is `StaleTransfer`. A subsequent call with the smaller `now_ms` `t + 10`
    is clamped and changes nothing. A `Begin` at `u64::MAX − 10` has deadline `u64::MAX`
    (saturating).
20. `expiry_frees_and_retry_publishes`: receiver A's transfer expires
    during a call on receiver B (the pool-wide sweep); the aggregate room
    comes back at once; A's `resync` is `Expired`, its `Resync` frame
    round-trips, and A's next id then publishes (cleanup and retry positive
    control).
21. `old_mixed_epoch_and_id_refusals`: a wrong-epoch `Begin`, chunk and
    `Cancel` are each `WrongEpoch` with the pool unchanged, and the
    in-flight transfer still publishes; a `Begin` at or below
    `retired_through` is `StaleTransfer`; a chunk for an id that never began
    is `UnknownTransfer`; a cursor with `cursor + 1 < next_event_seq` and a
    `base_tick` below the published one are `BehindReplica`; an equal
    `base_tick` is accepted.
22. `invalid_body_never_publishes`: a full 1 MiB opaque payload over 32
    chunks completes assembly and is `Failed(InvalidBody(_))` on chunk 32;
    the maximal body under `entity_count` 1,023 is
    `Failed(InvalidBody(Malformed))`; a body with a trailing byte too. In
    each, the earlier snapshot stays the only queued one and staging returns
    to 0.
23. `cutover_releases_in_fifo_order` (V2 receiver, `Live`): deltas 1..=3
    are released; `Begin(cursor 5)`; delta 5 is `Buffered`, then delta 4 is
    `Released { count: 2 }`; deltas 6 and 7 are `Buffered`; the last chunk
    is `Published { released: 2 }`. The queue is D1..D5, Snapshot, D6, D7.
    Repeats of 5 and 6 are `Superseded`. The oracle, applying in pop order,
    equals the expected view. Negative controls: applying D5 after the
    snapshot, or dropping D6, makes the oracle fail.
24. `await_snapshot_holds_until_publication`: an `AwaitSnapshot` receiver
    holds deltas 1..=3 and queues nothing. `Begin(cursor 2)` publishes with
    the queue Snapshot, D3. Delta 5 without 4 stays held, and delta 4 is
    then `Released { count: 2 }`.
25. `delta_hold_caps_and_overflow`: 128 held frames fit and the 129th is
    `Failed(Overflow)`, which discards and retires the in-flight transfer,
    clears held deltas, sets `resync` to `Overflow` and the mode to
    `AwaitSnapshot`. With `delta_bytes_per_receiver` 65,536, an exact fit
    is accepted and one byte more overflows; the same for the pool total
    across two receivers. A new snapshot then publishes and later deltas
    flow, but the gap left by the dropped delta is never released. A
    conflicting duplicate of a held delta is `Failed(Conflict)`.
26. `delta_sequence_and_wire_refusals`: `event_seq` 0 and `u64::MAX` are
    `DeltaSequence`, and `SEQ_LAST` is held; a v1 frame to a V2 receiver,
    and the reverse, is `Codec(UnsupportedVersion)`. Each leaves the pool
    unchanged.
27. `cancel_retires_and_supersedes`: `Cancel` of the in-flight transfer is
    `Cancelled { discarded: true }`, frees staging, sets `AwaitSnapshot` and
    leaves `resync` unset; its remaining chunks are `StaleTransfer`; a
    repeat is `Cancelled { discarded: false }`. `Cancel(9)` with nothing in
    flight retires through 9, `Begin(9)` is then `StaleTransfer` and
    `Begin(10)` publishes. Cancelling an already-published id leaves its
    queued snapshot in place.
28. `revocation_never_republishes_hidden_facts`: transfer 1 (6 chunks)
    discloses entity E's health. After 5 chunks the stand-in revokes and
    sends `Cancel(Aborted)`. The sixth chunk and duplicates of all six are
    `StaleTransfer`. Transfer 2, built under the narrowed grants,
    publishes, and the oracle finds E's health absent and every fact
    permitted. Negative control: without the cancel, chunk 6 publishes E's
    health and the oracle flags the leak.
29. `hidden_values_never_reach_bytes`: two fixtures that differ only in
    hidden values (an ungranted entity's health, an ungranted entity's
    presence, the turn while `turn_state` is not granted) produce
    byte-identical `plan_transfer` frames. A granted change produces
    different bytes (positive control). A `None` health is one `0` byte.
30. `oracle_catches_dropped_and_corrupt_chunks`: dropping a required chunk
    means no snapshot, and the oracle reports it missing. One health byte
    corrupted consistently (duplicates carry the same corruption) gives a
    decodable wrong body that publishes, and the oracle reports the
    mismatch. The unmodified transfer matches.
31. `fabric_transfer_with_faults_and_resync`: the stand-in sends through
    `InMemoryTransport` (`QueueCaps::v1_egress()`, test-generous rates, see

    loss that hits a chunk, the transfer expires at `t + 5,000`; the client
    encodes `Resync { after: Some(1), Expired }`, the stand-in decodes it and
    sends transfer 2, which publishes, and the oracle matches.

### S§16. Open questions (each with a recommendation)

1. **Wire identity.** Use a separate subprotocol (D11's selection) with
   first byte `0x81`, lane 2, downstream tags 0x10–0x12 and upstream tags
   0x20–0x21, recorded in ADR-0028 as Accepted with this approval. The
   alternative is a main-protocol v3, which D11 and the outline both
   rule out: it would mix combat and transfer vocabularies.
   *Recommendation:* approve as written. The disjoint first byte makes
   every v1/v2/transfer mix-up fail at the first byte, both ways.
2. **Packing and the full-size claim.** A valid v1 body is at most 22,552
   bytes, so the 1 MiB / 32-chunk envelope cannot be reached with a valid
   snapshot today (S§1.4). The contract proposes:
   - (a) proving the full envelope at the byte layer (case 10: exact
     1,048,576 bytes over 32 chunks), plus a full-size negative at the
     receiver (case 22);
   - (b) letting the sender choose 1–8 segments per chunk, so real
     multi-chunk publication is tested (6 chunks for the maximal body) and
     frames can stay small when sharing a stream with deltas.

   The alternative is a fixed 8 segments, where every valid body is one
   chunk and reorder, missing and duplicate chunks are never exercised with
   a valid body. Lowering the frozen caps instead would be a v1 constant
   change.
   *Recommendation:* approve (a) and (b).
3. **Ordering and cutover.** Correctness assumes a peer's transfer and
   delta frames share one total order, and the host never resets
   `event_seq` within an epoch (S§10.7, S§11 items 2–3). The receiver
   refuses (`BehindReplica`) rather than corrupt if the order breaks.
   Updates leave through one FIFO queue.
   *Recommendation:* approve. The transport child should carry transfer
   frames on the existing ordered stream with an explicit channel selector
   in the framing (not by inspecting payload bytes) under ALPN
   `crpg-lane0/2`. Separate streams would need a cross-stream barrier.
4. **Fail closed.** After expiry, conflict, an invalid body, overflow or a
   cancel, the receiver holds deltas until a new snapshot publishes. The
   alternative is to resume the delta stream when it has no gap. That saves
   a resync after a transient failure, but it can continue on a base the
   host meant to replace (narrowing).
   *Recommendation:* fail closed. The host answers `Resync` with a fresh
   snapshot, which D11 already requires.
5. **Follow-on tasks.** T024 alone carries no snapshot over a real
   connection. File two contract-needed rows when this contract is
   approved:
   - **T024q** (`crpg-net-quic`): the transfer channel under ALPN
     `crpg-lane0/2` (Q3);
   - **T024h** (`crpg-server`): S§11 and seamless narrowing.

   Both go before T025b, which needs them. T025a's contract can start
   once T024 is approved.
   *Recommendation:* approve filing both. Neither needs a new dependency:
   `crpg-net-quic` and `crpg-server` already have their edges.
